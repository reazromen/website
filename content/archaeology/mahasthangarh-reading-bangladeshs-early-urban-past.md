# Mahasthangarh: Reading Bangladesh’s Early Urban Past

Mahasthangarh is often introduced as one of Bangladesh’s earliest urban archaeological sites. That description is useful, but the more interesting question is what makes a place urban over such a long period: defensive boundaries, organised space, exchange, administration, religious life, or the ability to adapt as rivers and political centres change.

The site lies near Bogura, beside the Karatoya River in the historic region of Pundravardhana. Its fortified core is associated with ancient Pundranagara, a name known from historical and epigraphic evidence. The visible ramparts are only one part of the archaeological landscape. Mounds, exposed brickwork, excavated structures and finds from the wider area help researchers reconstruct how the settlement developed and how its surroundings were used.

## A city is more than a wall

A fortification can suggest that a settlement needed to mark or defend its boundary, but it does not by itself explain the whole society. Archaeologists examine the relationship between walls, gates, streets, buildings, pottery, coins, inscriptions and everyday objects. Each class of evidence answers a different question. A coin may indicate a monetary system or exchange network; pottery can help establish a sequence of occupation; an inscription may preserve a name, title or institutional relationship.

Mahasthangarh’s long sequence matters because the site should not be treated as a single city frozen in one moment. Political authority, religious practices, building materials and economic connections changed over centuries. The river landscape also changed, influencing routes and the relationship between the settlement and its hinterland.

## Reading evidence carefully

The archaeological record is incomplete. Excavation samples only part of a site, and later construction, erosion and cultivation can disturb earlier layers. A spectacular object is not automatically representative of ordinary life, while an absence of finds can reflect the limits of excavation rather than a genuine absence in the past. The strongest interpretation combines stratigraphy, artefact study, inscriptions, environmental context and comparison with other sites.

That is why Mahasthangarh is valuable beyond its age. It offers a way to investigate how an urban centre worked within the deltaic and river-connected world of Bengal, and how its importance shifted over time.

## What remains open

The broad identification with ancient Pundranagara gives the site a historical framework, but many details of urban life still depend on archaeological interpretation. How did neighbourhoods relate to the fortified core? Which routes mattered most in different periods? How did ordinary households experience political and religious change? Those questions require careful work across many kinds of evidence rather than a single origin story.

Mahasthangarh is therefore not simply a monument to an ancient capital. It is an archive of accumulated lives, and its value lies in keeping the questions testable.
