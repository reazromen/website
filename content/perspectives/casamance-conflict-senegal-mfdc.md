# The Casamance Conflict in Senegal: MFDC Factions and a Long Peace Process

The Casamance conflict in southern Senegal predates this archive’s 2000 start date, but armed activity and peace efforts continued into the period under review. The Movement of Democratic Forces of Casamance (MFDC) and associated factions have sought independence for the region, which is separated from much of northern Senegal by The Gambia. The conflict has waxed and waned rather than remaining at a constant intensity.

## Geography and political background

Casamance’s distinct history, regional identity, land issues and perceptions of marginalization contributed to separatist mobilization. MFDC has not been a perfectly unified organization: factions and commanders have differed over strategy, leadership and willingness to negotiate. A peace announcement by one faction does not automatically bind every armed actor.

## Fighting and peace efforts

Clashes between state forces and separatist fighters, landmines, security operations and local insecurity have affected residents, farming and movement. Cross-border routes and the geography of The Gambia and Guinea-Bissau have influenced the conflict. Historical incidents should not be used as evidence of current fighting without recent sources.

Senegalese authorities have pursued military operations and several rounds of dialogue. Local agreements and surrenders have produced progress in some areas, but disarmament and reintegration have been uneven. Negotiations do not prove that all factions have demobilized.

## Classification and sources

This entry focuses on the post-2000 phase while explaining the older origins. It distinguishes active clashes from crime, land disputes and peaceful political advocacy. Sources for verification include regional peace-process documentation, humanitarian reporting, International Crisis Group, UCDP and ACLED.
