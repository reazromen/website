# International Intervention in Libya in 2011

In 2011, an uprising against Muammar Gaddafi’s government escalated into armed conflict and international intervention. UN Security Council Resolution 1973 authorized measures to protect civilians, including a no-fly zone, and NATO led a campaign of air operations. The intervention contributed to the military outcome, but the collapse of the old state and later fragmentation cannot be explained by air power alone.

## Timeline and actors

Libya’s subsequent conflict involved local armed coalitions, rival governments, external sponsors and competition over institutions and resources. The 2011 intervention should therefore be separated analytically from the 2014–2020 civil-war phases and later localized fighting. External states’ roles changed across time, and support to Libyan actors needs evidence specific to each period.

## Scope and classification

This entry focuses on the 2011 intervention and its connection to Libya’s broader conflict. It is not a standalone war count. Legal authorization, conduct of hostilities, civilian casualties and the aftermath are separate questions and should be sourced independently.
