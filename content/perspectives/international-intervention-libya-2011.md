# International Intervention in Libya in 2011

The international intervention in Libya in 2011 took place during the uprising against Muammar Gaddafi’s government. As government forces advanced against opposition-held areas, the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 1973, authorizing measures to protect civilians, including a no-fly zone. NATO then led the military operation commonly known as Operation Unified Protector.

## Mandate and conduct

The intervention included air and maritime operations and support for enforcing the arms embargo and no-fly zone. NATO and participating governments described their actions as necessary to protect civilians. Critics argued that the operation’s practical effects went beyond a narrow civilian-protection mission and helped opposition forces defeat the government. Those interpretations should be presented as competing analyses, while operational facts and the wording of the UN mandate should be documented separately.

The intervention was not the only cause of the regime’s collapse: domestic opposition, defections, local armed groups and the government’s own decisions were central. At the same time, foreign air power materially affected the battlefield. A rigorous account avoids both treating Libya’s uprising as purely foreign-made and ignoring the intervention’s military importance.

## After 2011

The fall of Gaddafi did not produce a stable monopoly of force. Armed groups, rival institutions and external patrons shaped later conflicts, including the 2014–2020 wars and subsequent political fragmentation. Those later periods should not be folded into the 2011 intervention as though the same operation continued indefinitely.
