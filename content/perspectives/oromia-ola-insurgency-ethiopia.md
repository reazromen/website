# The Oromia Conflict: Oromo Liberation Army and Ethiopian State Forces

The armed conflict in Oromia involves the Oromo Liberation Army (OLA), Ethiopian federal and regional security forces, and a broader political landscape shaped by long-standing disputes over representation, land, state power and the place of Oromo nationalism. The OLA emerged from a split within the Oromo Liberation Front (OLF) and has operated as an armed insurgent organization. The conflict should be distinguished from the Tigray war and the Amhara armed conflict, even where political developments connect them.

## Background and armed campaign

Oromo political movements have a long history, including periods of legal opposition and armed resistance. The OLF returned from exile during Ethiopia’s political opening in 2018, but disagreements over disarmament, political participation and security contributed to fragmentation. The armed group known as the OLA continued operations against state forces and was designated a terrorist organization by the Ethiopian parliament in 2021; such legal designations should be identified as government actions rather than substitutes for independent analysis.

Clashes, ambushes and attacks have occurred in parts of Oromia, while government forces have conducted security operations. Local conflicts over land and authority can overlap with the insurgency, but not every incident in Oromia is attributable to the OLA. Actor attribution should rely on corroborated reporting and distinguish claimed responsibility from verified responsibility.

## Civilian harm and contested narratives

Civilians have faced killings, abductions, displacement, restrictions on movement and interruptions to schooling and healthcare. Reports of massacres or abuses have sometimes been contested, and communication barriers have complicated independent access. The archive should not repeat a party’s accusation as fact without corroboration, but should not omit credible investigations simply because a party disputes them.

## Relationship to national politics

Oromia is Ethiopia’s largest region by population and has substantial political significance. The insurgency intersects with debates about federalism, autonomy, political competition and the role of security forces. These underlying disputes help explain context but do not justify attacks on civilians or establish responsibility for particular incidents.
