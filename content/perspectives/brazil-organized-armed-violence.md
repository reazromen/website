# Brazil’s Organized Armed Violence

Brazil has experienced extensive organized lethal violence involving criminal factions, local armed groups, prison networks and security forces. The Primeiro Comando da Capital and Comando Vermelho are influential in different regions, while local militias and rival groups have shaped territorial control in parts of the country. Police operations and criminal rivalries have produced severe civilian consequences.

## Timeline and actors

A high homicide rate alone does not establish an armed conflict. The relevant questions are whether identifiable organized groups engage in sustained fighting, whether state forces are a party to a particular confrontation, and whether event-level evidence supports a conflict classification. The actors and dynamics differ between the Amazon region, major metropolitan areas and prison systems.

## Scope and classification

This article is a framework for assessing organized violence, not a claim that Brazil is one continuous war. Any standalone case should define its geographic limits and actors, distinguish police lethality from group-on-group fighting, and use transparent sources for casualties and attribution.
