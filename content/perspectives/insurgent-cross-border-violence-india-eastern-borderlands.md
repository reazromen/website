# Insurgent and Cross-Border Violence in India’s Eastern Borderlands

India’s eastern borderlands contain several distinct armed histories rather than one continuous insurgency. Nagaland, Manipur, Assam and Tripura have hosted organizations with different political aims, support networks, ceasefire arrangements and levels of activity. Border connections with Myanmar have affected movement, sanctuary and security operations, but geography alone does not make these campaigns a single conflict.

## Timeline and actors

A useful chronology separates the Naga political conflict, armed activity in Manipur, ULFA-linked campaigns in Assam and Tripura-based organizations. Each has its own negotiations and splinter groups. Ceasefires can reduce violence without resolving sovereignty, autonomy, demobilization or accountability questions. The post-2021 conflict in Myanmar has also altered cross-border conditions, but should not be used to assume that every border incident belongs to a new interstate war.

## Scope and classification

This essay is a regional guide, not a claim that all groups remain active or that the region constitutes one UCDP conflict. The archive should attach individual events to named actors and years, and distinguish insurgent attacks, security operations, communal violence and political negotiations.
