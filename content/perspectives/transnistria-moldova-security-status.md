# Transnistria and Moldova: The Post-2000 Security Status

Transnistria is a breakaway region of Moldova whose de facto authorities have operated separately from the internationally recognized Moldovan government since the conflict of 1992. A ceasefire and Russian military presence have shaped the post-war security environment. Since 2000, the region has remained a significant political and strategic issue, but that does not mean continuous active war.

## A frozen conflict with changing risks

The 1992 fighting falls outside the archive’s 2000 start date. Subsequent years have been marked by negotiations, unresolved status questions, the presence of Russian troops and ammunition stocks, and wider geopolitical tensions. Moldova’s constitutional status and Transnistria’s de facto institutions remain disputed. These are important security facts, but political tension and military presence alone do not establish ongoing lethal armed conflict.

Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 changed the regional context and increased concern about security risks around Moldova. Specific incidents or alleged plots should be assessed using dated evidence and reliable attribution rather than assumed to be part of an active war in Transnistria.

## Classification and archive scope

The archive should distinguish the historical 1992 conflict, the post-ceasefire status dispute, Russian military presence and any discrete post-2000 incidents that meet an armed-violence threshold. A “frozen conflict” is a political description, not a guarantee that fighting is active or a dataset category by itself.

## What remains to be checked

A final dataset reconciliation should examine whether UCDP records any qualifying state-based or other organized lethal conflict in Moldova after 2000 and whether individual incidents are attributable to the Transnistrian theatre. If the evidence does not support an active armed-conflict entry, this page should remain a security-context article rather than be counted as a war.
