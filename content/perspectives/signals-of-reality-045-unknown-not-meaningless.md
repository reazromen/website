A sensor produces a repeating pulse every afternoon.

The instrument passes its self-test. The pulse is not in the design documents. Nobody on the team can explain it.

What should we call it?

Unknown is accurate.

Meaningless is not.

That distinction seems small, but it changes how investigations behave. Unknown describes the state of knowledge. Meaningless makes a claim about the phenomenon itself.

The first can be revised by evidence. The second often ends the search before evidence has had a chance to accumulate.

## A residual can contain structure

In measurement, a residual is what remains after a model's prediction is subtracted from an observation. Residuals are often treated as noise, and frequently that is reasonable.

But a good residual analysis asks whether the remainder behaves like the assumed noise model.

Does it have a daily cycle? Does it correlate with temperature? Does it appear only after a firmware update? Does it line up with another sensor? Does its variance change under load?

A regular structure in residuals is information about a mismatch between model and observation.

It does not tell us which side is wrong.

The model may be incomplete. The instrument may be drifting. A hidden environmental variable may be present. A data-processing bug may exist. The structure is a clue, not a diagnosis.

## Unknown causes can still make predictions

Suppose a team does not know why the afternoon pulse occurs, but after several weeks they can predict its timing within a narrow window.

That predictability is already knowledge.

They may also learn that the pulse disappears when a nearby machine is powered down, or shifts when the sensor is moved, or remains unchanged when the data logger is replaced.

Each intervention reduces the space of explanations.

The final mechanism can remain unknown while the pattern becomes increasingly constrained.

Science often advances this way. A phenomenon can be measured and characterized before a complete theory explains it. Classification, regularity, and reproducibility are not substitutes for mechanism, but they are not nothing.

## Meaning depends on a question

There is also a second sense of meaning.

A pattern can have no obvious semantic message and still carry information.

Claude Shannon's communication theory deliberately separated statistical information from semantic content. A sequence can have measurable uncertainty and structure without "meaning" anything in the ordinary human sense. His 1948 paper, [A Mathematical Theory of Communication](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x), is foundational precisely because it makes this separation useful.

A timing pattern in network traffic may not contain readable words, yet it can reveal load, protocol state, or user behavior. A spectral line is not a sentence, yet it can support an inference about matter. A regular heartbeat waveform does not contain a linguistic message, yet its structure matters.

Semantic meaning is not the only kind of usable relation.

## The danger of filling the gap too quickly

Unknown does not mean meaningless, but it also does not mean mysterious in the dramatic sense.

When people dislike an unexplained pattern, they may jump from "we do not know" to "there must be a hidden extraordinary cause."

That is the mirror-image error.

An unexplained radio burst is not automatically an alien signal. An unexplained sensor spike is not automatically a new physical phenomenon. An unexplained correlation is not automatically causal.

The correct state can remain genuinely unresolved.

This is intellectually uncomfortable because uncertainty does not make a good headline. But keeping the unknown category open protects both directions: it prevents premature dismissal and premature celebration.

## A useful unknown has documentation

An unknown becomes scientifically valuable when it is described well enough to investigate.

That means preserving timestamps, raw measurements, sensor configuration, environmental conditions, software versions, calibration state, and processing steps. It means distinguishing what was observed from what was inferred.

"Something weird happened" is difficult to reproduce.

"At 14:05 local time, channel 3 increased by 18 percent for 1.2 seconds in 17 of 20 days; the event coincided with a current spike on the adjacent power monitor" is a starting point.

The second statement still may not explain anything. It turns ignorance into a testable object.

## Unknown is a temporary label, not a failure

There is a cultural temptation to treat "I don't know" as embarrassment. In engineering and science, it can be one of the most accurate outputs available.

The point is not to admire ignorance. The point is to locate it precisely.

We know the pulse exists in the recorded data. We know the conditions under which it appears. We know some explanations fail. We do not yet know the mechanism.

That is a structured state of knowledge.

Unknown does not mean meaningless because the world can contain regularities before our concepts catch up.

The discipline is to keep the observation, keep the uncertainty, and resist inventing the missing explanation.
