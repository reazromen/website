Start with hydrogen and helium.

End with DNA, proteins, membranes, metabolism, and cells.

The gap is so large that a sentence like "we are made of star stuff" can make the transition sound almost automatic.

It was not.

The path crosses several distinct scientific problems.

Cosmology.

Stellar nucleosynthesis.

Astrochemistry.

Planet formation.

Geochemistry.

Prebiotic chemistry.

Evolution.

Each layer creates possibilities for the next.

None guarantees it.

## The universe first needed elements

Primordial nucleosynthesis produced mostly hydrogen and helium.

Stars later produced carbon, oxygen, nitrogen, silicon, iron, and many other elements through several astrophysical processes.

Without those elements, familiar biochemistry could not exist.

The periodic table had to become chemically rich enough.

That is the first bottleneck.

## Interstellar space built molecules

Enriched gas did not remain atomic.

Molecular clouds formed H₂, CO, water, methanol, ammonia, and many organic species.

Dust-grain chemistry and radiation processing created more complexity.

Some of this material entered protoplanetary disks.

Chemistry began before planets.

But molecules are not metabolism.

Complexity is not life.

## Planets created concentrated environments

A planet provides something diffuse space often does not:

persistent interfaces.

Liquid phases.

Mineral surfaces.

Atmospheres.

Hydrothermal gradients.

Wet-dry cycles.

UV exposure.

Localized chemistry.

These environments can concentrate molecules and create reaction networks that would be improbable in extremely dilute space.

Planetary geology becomes part of prebiotic chemistry.

## Origin-of-life chemistry remains an open problem

Researchers study plausible routes from simple molecules to self-sustaining chemical systems capable of heredity and evolution.

RNA-related chemistry.

Metabolic networks.

Membrane formation.

Autocatalysis.

Mineral catalysis.

No complete historical reconstruction of Earth's first life is established.

That uncertainty should remain visible.

A cosmic inventory of ingredients is not an origin-of-life theory.

## Evolution changes the regime

Once heritable systems reproduce with variation and differential success, Darwinian evolution becomes possible.

Now chemistry can accumulate adaptation across generations.

This is a profound transition.

Before evolution, reaction networks depend on chemistry and environment.

After evolution, lineages can acquire increasingly elaborate mechanisms because successful variants persist.

Information becomes historical in a new way.

## Life does not escape chemistry

Cells are made of ordinary atoms.

Their reactions obey thermodynamics.

Their membranes, proteins, nucleic acids, ions, and water are physical matter.

What changes is organization.

The same elements participate in a self-maintaining, reproducing, evolving network.

The jump from nonlife to life is therefore not a change in fundamental substance.

It is a change in system dynamics.

## No straight line was guaranteed

A cloud does not contain a hidden embryo of life.

Most matter never becomes biological.

Most planets may never become inhabited.

Many chemical paths terminate.

Cosmic history provides ingredients and environments, not purpose.

This is where teleological storytelling should stop.

From hydrogen cloud to living chemistry is not one mechanism.

It is a chain of enabling transitions.

Each stage inherits constraints from the previous one and creates new possibilities.

Life is part of cosmic history not because the universe aimed toward it, but because ordinary matter can pass through extraordinary levels of organization.
