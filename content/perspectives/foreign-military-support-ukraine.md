# Foreign Military Support in Ukraine Since 2014

Foreign military support has been a defining feature of the Russo-Ukrainian war, particularly after Russia’s 2014 seizure of Crimea and the fighting in Donbas, and after the full-scale invasion launched in February 2022. Support has included weapons, ammunition, training, intelligence, financial assistance, logistics and sanctions-related measures. These activities affect the war but should not automatically be counted as separate conflicts.

## Support to Ukraine

The type and scale of assistance from Ukraine’s partners have changed over time. Different states have supplied different systems under national rules and political constraints. Training missions and equipment deliveries should be distinguished from direct participation in combat. Claims about battlefield effects require caution because they are often based on official statements, incomplete data or wartime information controls.

## Support to Russia

Russia has also obtained external material and technical support, with public reporting and government assessments identifying relationships involving ammunition, drones and other supplies. The exact nature of particular transfers, the legal status of assistance and the degree of operational coordination need case-specific sources. Providing equipment does not automatically make a supplier a direct combatant under every analytical or legal framework.

## Why this is a separate archive layer

The primary conflict remains the war between Russia and Ukraine. External support is a relationship across that conflict, not a second war occurring in parallel. If foreign forces directly engage in combat, those events should be documented distinctly and according to the applicable definition. The archive should also distinguish military aid from humanitarian support, financial assistance and diplomatic alignment.

## Archive treatment

This page is a cross-index linking the Russo-Ukrainian war, Donbas and the 2022 full-scale invasion. It should track support by country, type and period, while avoiding the double-counting of the same battlefield events.
