# Paraguay’s EPP Insurgency: A Localized Armed Campaign

The Paraguayan People’s Army (Ejército del Pueblo Paraguayo, EPP) is a small armed organization associated with attacks, kidnappings and clashes with Paraguayan security forces, particularly in northern departments. The campaign is geographically limited compared with the country as a whole, and its level of activity has varied over time.

## Localized campaign and state response

The EPP emerged in the late 2000s and has been linked by Paraguayan authorities to left-wing insurgent aims. The group’s size, internal organization and relationship to other actors should be described using reliable evidence rather than official labels alone. State operations have targeted suspected members and support networks. Each incident requires attribution because allegations of membership or support can be contested.

Kidnappings, killings and attacks on security personnel are not identical categories. The archive should distinguish confirmed incidents, alleged EPP activity, criminal acts and broader security operations. It should also avoid assuming that every violent event in northern Paraguay belongs to the EPP campaign.

## Classification questions

A named armed organization and a series of attacks do not automatically establish that a conflict is active in every year. A rigorous chronology needs documented events, organization, parties and a defensible threshold for lethal violence. Where the available record is sparse or disputed, uncertainty should be explicit.

## Archive treatment

This article treats the EPP as a localized campaign requiring year-by-year review. It does not describe Paraguay as a country-wide war. The next dataset audit should compare reported events with UCDP’s conflict definitions and independent reporting, while keeping the group’s existence separate from proof of continuous combat.
