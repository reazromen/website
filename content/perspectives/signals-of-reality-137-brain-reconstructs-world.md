Your brain has never touched the Moon.

It has never touched the tree outside the window.

In the ordinary sensory sense, it does not touch the light either.

Photons interact with receptors in the retina.

Air pressure moves structures in the ear.

Molecules bind receptors in the nose.

Skin deforms.

Those interactions become neural signals.

The brain operates on the consequences.

## Perception begins with transduction

A sensory receptor converts one form of physical variation into biological activity.

The neural signal is not the original object.

A spike train is not red light.

Activity in the auditory nerve is not air pressure.

A pattern of somatosensory firing is not the table surface.

Yet these signals preserve relationships to the world strongly enough for organisms to act.

That is the central miracle of perception: mediated contact can still be reliable contact.

## The input is ambiguous

A pattern on the retina can be produced by different three-dimensional scenes.

The same sound pressure at one ear can originate from different positions unless information from time, intensity, and the other ear is combined.

A shadow can result from object color or illumination.

Sensory data underdetermines cause.

The nervous system must use context, movement, prior structure, and multiple senses to infer likely sources.

Perception is an inverse problem.

## Reconstruction does not mean fabrication

The word *construct* is easily abused.

If perception is constructed, some people jump to:

reality is whatever the brain decides.

No.

The reconstruction is constrained by incoming signals and by the consequences of action.

Walk toward the inferred wall and the wall pushes back.

Reach for the cup and tactile feedback confirms or corrects the visual estimate.

Move your head and parallax changes predictably.

The model survives because the world constrains it.

## Illusions reveal assumptions

Visual illusions are valuable not because they show vision is useless.

They reveal the assumptions the system normally uses successfully.

Context.

Lighting.

Perspective.

Motion.

Contrast.

When an artificial stimulus exploits those assumptions, perception can diverge from physical measurement.

The error teaches us about the inference process.

A receiver can be reliable in its natural environment while failing on carefully designed edge cases.

## Action improves perception

Organisms do not merely wait for data.

Eyes move.

Heads turn.

Hands touch.

Bodies approach.

These actions change the signal.

Active sensing reduces ambiguity.

A robot does the same when it moves a camera to get parallax or directs a radar beam.

Perception is coupled to action.

The observer participates in acquiring evidence.

## The brain integrates several imperfect views

Vision.

Hearing.

Vestibular signals.

Touch.

Proprioception.

Each has different resolution, latency, and failure modes.

Agreement among them can increase confidence.

Disagreement produces interesting effects such as sensory recalibration and illusions.

Again, no one channel contains the world.

The nervous system combines partial signals.

## There is no inner screen

Saying the brain "reconstructs an image" can create another homunculus problem.

Who looks at the image?

Neuroscience does not need a miniature observer inside the head.

Perception is the coordinated activity that supports discrimination, action, report, memory, and experience.

The representation is distributed biological state, not necessarily a literal picture.

## A world never touched directly can still be known

Scientific instruments extend the same principle.

A telescope turns photons into electrons.

A spectrometer turns wavelength structure into detector counts.

Software turns counts into plots.

The scientist infers a star.

Mediation is not the enemy of realism.

Characterized mediation is how evidence works.

The brain reconstructs a world it never touches directly.

The remarkable part is not that perception is mediated.

It is how effectively a living system can infer stable external structure from signals that are always partial.
