# Boko Haram and the Lake Chad Basin Conflict

The Boko Haram insurgency began in northeastern Nigeria and escalated sharply in 2009 after a confrontation with state security forces. Over time, the movement developed into a regional conflict affecting Cameroon, Chad and Niger around the Lake Chad Basin. The insurgency later fragmented, including the emergence of an Islamic State-aligned faction commonly known as Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP). These groups have overlapping histories but distinct leadership and operational patterns.

## From local movement to insurgency

Boko Haram grew amid religious, economic and political tensions in northeastern Nigeria. After the group’s 2009 uprising and the killing of its founder, Mohammed Yusuf, it reorganized under Abubakar Shekau and launched increasingly lethal attacks. It targeted security forces, government institutions and civilians, including through bombings, village raids, abductions and the mass kidnapping of schoolgirls from Chibok in 2014.

In 2015, regional governments intensified military cooperation through the Multinational Joint Task Force. Boko Haram pledged allegiance to the Islamic State in 2015, but internal disputes later produced a split. ISWAP and the Shekau-led faction competed for influence, and their treatment of civilians and relations with local communities differed by period. The names should not be used interchangeably when evidence identifies a specific faction.

## Regional expansion and civilian impact

Attacks and military operations crossed borders, displacing millions of people and disrupting farming, fishing, trade and education. Civilians faced killings, forced recruitment, sexual violence and abduction by armed groups, as well as risks from military operations and movement restrictions. The lake’s ecology, economic pressures and weak service provision affect community resilience, but they do not explain every decision made by armed organizations.

Humanitarian access is difficult in insecure areas, and official casualty claims can be incomplete or politically framed. Reliable reporting should distinguish combatants from civilians and identify whether a figure covers a single incident, a district or a broader period.

## Conflict classification

This entry treats the Lake Chad Basin insurgency as a regional theatre with separate national incidents and faction-specific records. It cross-links to Nigeria’s wider security situation but does not classify all violence in the region as Boko Haram-related.
