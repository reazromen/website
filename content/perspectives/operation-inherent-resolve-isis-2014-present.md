# Operation Inherent Resolve Against the Islamic State, 2014 Onward

Operation Inherent Resolve (OIR) is the name used for the US-led coalition campaign against the Islamic State (ISIS/ISIL), launched in 2014 as the organization seized territory in Iraq and Syria. The campaign combined air operations, training, intelligence, logistics and support to local forces. Its military scope and partner relationships changed over time.

## Iraq and Syria are related but distinct theatres

In Iraq, the coalition supported Iraqi security forces and Kurdish forces in operations to reverse ISIS territorial control, including the battles to retake major cities. The Iraqi state’s conflict with ISIS developed from earlier insurgency and political fragmentation. In Syria, coalition operations intersected with the Syrian civil war, Kurdish-led forces, Turkish operations, government forces, opposition groups and other external actors. These overlapping theatres should not be treated as a single undifferentiated battlefield.

The territorial defeat of ISIS’s self-declared caliphate did not mean that all violence ended. The group and affiliated organizations continued to conduct insurgent attacks in various locations. The level of activity, the parties and the role of coalition forces have varied by year and country. A statement that ISIS was “defeated” must therefore specify whether it refers to territorial control, military capacity or the organization’s continued existence.

## Evidence and civilian harm

Coalition statements, local partner reporting, independent investigations and human-rights organizations have provided different types of evidence about operations and civilian casualties. Claims should be attributed and dated. The archive should distinguish confirmed civilian deaths from allegations, and should not conflate all airstrikes in Iraq or Syria with OIR without checking the operation and participants.

## Archive treatment

This page records OIR as an intervention campaign linked to the Iraq and Syria essays. It is not an additional war count. The archive should separate coalition actions from local conflict dynamics and maintain a timeline of mission changes and partner forces.
