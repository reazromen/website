A neuron fires.

Or it does not.

That sounds binary.

From far enough away, it is tempting to describe the brain as a gigantic network of biological bits.

The analogy captures one feature of action potentials.

It misses most of the system.

## Membrane voltage is continuous

A neuron's membrane potential changes continuously.

Synaptic inputs can depolarize or hyperpolarize the membrane by graded amounts.

Dendrites integrate signals over space and time.

The cell contains a dynamic electrical state before any spike occurs.

The action potential is an event generated when membrane dynamics cross conditions that trigger regenerative ion-channel opening.

The existence of a spike does not make the entire neuron binary.

## Action potentials are stereotyped, not identical bits

Once initiated, action potentials have a characteristic shape.

They do not usually encode signal strength by becoming proportionally taller in the simple way an analog amplifier would.

But information can appear in spike timing.

Rate.

Burst pattern.

Synchrony.

Latency.

Population activity.

Which neuron fired.

A binary event becomes part of a much richer code over time and networks.

## Synapses are not fixed wires

The effect of one neuron's spike on another depends on the synapse.

Excitatory or inhibitory.

Strong or weak.

Facilitating or depressing.

Modified by neuromodulators.

Changed by learning and plasticity.

A spike arriving at one synapse does not have a universal downstream meaning.

The receiving context matters.

## Dendrites perform nonlinear operations

Dendrites were once simplified as passive cables summing inputs.

Modern neuroscience shows far richer behavior.

Voltage-dependent channels, local spikes, branch-specific integration, and morphology can create nonlinear processing before the cell body generates an action potential.

A neuron is not a logic gate with thousands of simple inputs.

It is a spatially extended biological system.

## Refractory periods create memory

After an action potential, ion-channel states change.

The neuron temporarily becomes less able to fire again.

Recent activity influences current responsiveness.

Adaptation can change firing over longer periods.

The neuron's response therefore depends on history.

A stateless on/off model cannot capture this.

## Networks add another layer

A single neuron's meaning depends on population context.

The same firing rate can participate in different network states.

Oscillations can synchronize activity.

Inhibition shapes timing.

Feedback loops create persistent states.

Behavior emerges from interactions across many neurons and other cell types.

The bit metaphor becomes less useful as scale increases.

## Digital and analog are both partial metaphors

Neural systems contain event-like spikes and continuous variables.

Molecular concentrations.

Membrane potentials.

Synaptic weights.

Timing intervals.

Neuromodulatory states.

There is no need to force the brain into one category.

Biology comfortably mixes discrete events with continuous dynamics.

## The metaphor still has a role

Simplified neurons are useful in computational models.

Binary units can reveal principles.

Spiking models capture other properties.

Biophysical models capture more detail at higher cost.

A model is useful when matched to the question.

The mistake is treating one abstraction as anatomy.

A neuron is not simply on or off.

It is a living dynamical system whose spike is one visible punctuation mark in a much longer electrical and chemical sentence.
