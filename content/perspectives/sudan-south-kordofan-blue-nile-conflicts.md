# Sudan’s South Kordofan and Blue Nile Conflicts: Separate Fronts, Connected Histories

South Kordofan and Blue Nile have experienced armed conflict connected to Sudan’s centre–periphery tensions, the legacy of the north–south civil war and disputes over political inclusion and security. The Sudan People’s Liberation Movement–North (SPLM-N) and its armed forces have been central actors, although factions and alliances changed. These theatres are distinct from Darfur and from the nationwide war between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that began in April 2023.

## Roots and renewed fighting

Communities in the Nuba Mountains of South Kordofan and in Blue Nile had relationships with the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement during Sudan’s earlier civil war. The 2005 Comprehensive Peace Agreement and South Sudan’s 2011 independence did not resolve every question about political representation, security arrangements and the status of these areas. Fighting resumed in 2011 between Sudanese government forces and SPLM-N, with displacement and restrictions on humanitarian access.

SPLM-N later divided into factions with different leadership and political alignments. Those distinctions became more consequential as Sudan’s national conflict transformed alliances. A report about one faction should not be generalized to the entire movement.

## Humanitarian impact and overlapping wars

Residents have faced displacement, disrupted agriculture, food insecurity and barriers to medical care and education. The 2023 SAF–RSF war created a wider national crisis and may affect these regions through shifting alliances, weapons flows and local operations. That does not make the earlier conflicts identical to the national war.

This entry keeps South Kordofan and Blue Nile as linked but distinct theatres, cross-referenced to the north–south war, Darfur and the 2023 war. Sources include UN humanitarian and human-rights reporting, International Crisis Group, UCDP and ACLED.
