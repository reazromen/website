# Transnational Islamic State Campaigns and Affiliates

The Islamic State (ISIS/ISIL) emerged as a territorial organization in Iraq and Syria and later developed or claimed affiliates in several regions. The organization’s central leadership, local branches and self-declared provinces have not always had the same capabilities or command relationships. An archive should therefore treat transnational activity as a network of related theatres rather than one global battlefield.

## From territorial control to insurgency

The loss of territorial control in Iraq and Syria changed the organization’s methods but did not end all of its activity. Affiliates or aligned organizations have operated in parts of Africa, South and Central Asia and elsewhere, with their own local conflicts and political conditions. Some groups have publicly pledged allegiance; others have been described as affiliates by governments or researchers. These labels should be supported by evidence and dated because alliances can change.

An attack claimed by ISIS is not automatically proof that the central organization planned or directed it. Analysts should distinguish central command, local affiliate operations, inspired attacks and opportunistic claims of responsibility. The same care applies to casualty figures and claims about territory.

## Separate conflict records

The Iraq and Syria campaign, Islamic State West Africa Province, Islamic State affiliates in the Sahel and other regional cases differ in actors, intensity and chronology. Their links matter, but each theatre should be documented on its own terms. A transnational label is useful for explaining ideological and organizational relationships; it should not duplicate every local event.

## Archive treatment

This page is a cross-index that connects country-specific articles. It should identify the source for each claimed affiliate relationship and avoid assuming a single chain of command. The archive should distinguish state-based conflict, non-state conflict, one-sided violence and terrorism incident data because these datasets use different definitions.
