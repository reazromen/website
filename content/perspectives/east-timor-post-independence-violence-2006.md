# East Timor’s Post-Independence Violence in 2006

Timor-Leste’s 2006 crisis emerged from divisions within the security forces, political conflict and wider institutional weakness. Dismissed soldiers, police factions, armed groups and political actors became entangled in violence that displaced large numbers of people and threatened the young state’s stability.

## Timeline and actors

The crisis was not simply a continuation of the 1999 independence violence. Its immediate dynamics centred on the post-independence security sector and political leadership. International forces and UN support helped restore security, while investigations and reform efforts addressed parts of the institutional breakdown. The event exposed the risks of unresolved factional tensions within state institutions.

## Scope and classification

This entry treats 2006 as a time-bounded crisis and does not describe all post-independence years as war. Classification depends on the organization and lethality of specific episodes. Displacement, political disorder and human-rights abuses are important evidence, but should be recorded separately from a claim that every episode meets a formal armed-conflict threshold.
