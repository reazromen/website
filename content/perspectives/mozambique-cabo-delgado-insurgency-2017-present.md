# Mozambique’s Cabo Delgado Insurgency: Local Grievances and Regional Intervention

Armed attacks began in Mozambique’s northern Cabo Delgado province in 2017 and developed into a sustained insurgency. The armed movement is locally known as al-Shabaab, but it is not the same organization as Somalia’s al-Shabaab. It later became associated with Islamic State’s Central Africa Province. The conflict has involved Mozambican forces, regional troops, foreign security personnel at different times, and civilians caught between armed actors.

## Origins and expansion

Cabo Delgado has significant natural resources, including offshore gas, while many communities have faced poverty, limited services and few economic opportunities. These conditions provide context but do not fully explain recruitment or violence. Insurgents attacked towns, villages and infrastructure, killing and abducting civilians and forcing residents to flee. The 2021 attack on Palma drew international attention to the conflict’s proximity to major gas projects.

## Regional intervention and civilian harm

Mozambique’s military response has been supplemented by forces from Rwanda and the Southern African Development Community at different times. The timing, mandate and conduct of deployments should be documented separately. A reopened district or changed troop presence does not prove the insurgency has ended.

The conflict has displaced residents and disrupted education, health care, farming and fishing. Claims about territorial control and casualty totals should be dated and independently checked. Allegations of abuses by insurgents and state or allied forces require credible investigation.

## Classification and sources

This entry tracks Cabo Delgado and its cross-border intervention links, distinct from Mozambique’s older RENAMO conflict and other Islamic State theatres. Sources include UN humanitarian reporting, UN Security Council reports, International Crisis Group, UCDP and ACLED.
