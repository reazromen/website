The cosmic microwave background did not begin as microwaves in the modern sense.

When the radiation decoupled from matter, the universe was thousands of kelvin.

Today the CMB has an almost perfect blackbody spectrum corresponding to about 2.7 K.

What happened?

The universe expanded.

The radiation stretched with it.

## Wavelength follows expansion

In an expanding universe described by standard cosmology, freely propagating photon wavelengths scale with the cosmic scale factor.

As space expands between successive wave crests, the observed wavelength grows.

Longer wavelength means lower photon energy.

This is cosmological redshift.

The CMB has been traveling while the universe expanded enormously.

Radiation once associated with a hot thermal field now arrives at microwave wavelengths.

## It is not ordinary cooling

Imagine a cup of coffee cooling.

Molecules transfer energy to the environment.

That is not the best picture for the CMB after decoupling.

The photons are not cooling mainly by colliding with colder matter.

Their wavelengths are stretched by cosmic expansion.

The temperature parameter of the blackbody radiation falls correspondingly.

The spectrum retains the blackbody form with a lower temperature.

## The spectrum is powerful evidence

NASA's COBE mission measured the CMB spectrum with extraordinary precision and found it extremely close to a blackbody.

That matters.

A hot early universe predicts a thermal radiation background.

The expansion predicts redshifting of that background.

The observed spectrum fits that history remarkably well.

The CMB is not merely a diffuse glow.

Its spectral shape is a quantitative fossil.

## Microwave is an observer-era label

Electromagnetic radiation does not know that humans divide the spectrum into radio, microwave, infrared, and visible bands.

Those categories describe wavelength ranges and technologies.

The same photon population changed category over cosmic time because its characteristic wavelength changed.

What was once much shorter-wavelength thermal radiation became microwave radiation for us.

The label belongs to the epoch of observation.

## Redshift is also a clock

If we observe a distant galaxy with spectral lines shifted by a known factor, that redshift tells us about cosmic expansion between emission and observation.

For the CMB, the redshift is enormous.

We are receiving radiation from an era when the scale factor was roughly a thousand times smaller than today.

The exact cosmological interpretation comes from the full model, but the core relation is simple:

more expansion means longer observed wavelength.

## Expansion preserves structure while changing scale

The CMB's wavelengths stretched.

Its anisotropy pattern also projected onto the growing universe.

Statistical properties of that pattern still encode conditions from the early plasma.

A signal can therefore change dramatically in physical scale while preserving information about its origin.

This happens elsewhere too.

A recording can be resampled.

A waveform can be frequency shifted.

A signal's present carrier properties do not have to match its original ones exactly for historical structure to survive.

## Microwave telescopes read ancient heat

Today, instruments detect the CMB at microwave and millimeter wavelengths.

Foreground radiation from our galaxy and other sources has to be separated.

Different frequency channels help distinguish components.

The final cosmological map is extracted from a sky full of overlapping emissions.

Why is the CMB microwave today?

Because the universe expanded while the photons traveled.

The radiation is old.

Its wavelength is a record of the space it crossed.
