# Other Documented African Non-State Conflicts

Non-state armed conflict involves organized armed groups fighting one another without a state government being a party to that particular dyad. In Africa, such violence has included communal militias, pastoralist and farmer-associated armed groups, local defence formations and competing insurgent organizations. The category is not synonymous with every communal dispute or criminal homicide.

## Timeline and actors

The UCDP Non-State Conflict Dataset provides a systematic starting point for identifying group-to-group fighting. Each candidate must then be reviewed for actor identity, years of activity, location, fatality threshold and relationship to any state-based war. The same armed organization may participate in more than one dyad, while a broad community label may conceal several independent groups.

## Scope and classification

This article is a method-led catalogue entry rather than a claim that all African non-state conflicts are covered here. Individual essays should be created only after each candidate has been checked against the underlying records and independent reporting. Where evidence is thin, the archive should say so rather than infer a conflict from country-level violence totals.
