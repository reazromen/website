# Peru and the Remnants of Sendero Luminoso

Peru’s internal armed conflict of the 1980s and 1990s caused extensive civilian suffering, but the archive’s 2000–2026 window requires a narrower account of the remnants that persisted in remote areas. Armed factions associated with Sendero Luminoso continued to operate in parts of the Apurímac, Ene and Mantaro River Valley, where coca economies, difficult terrain and weak state presence shaped security conditions.

## Timeline and actors

These factions should not automatically be treated as a unified continuation of the original organization. Their leadership, political activity and links to illicit economies changed over time. Peruvian security operations and attacks attributed to armed remnants need to be dated and evaluated separately from the broader legacy of the historical conflict.

## Scope and classification

This article focuses on post-2000 armed activity and the difficulty of distinguishing ideological insurgency, criminal financing and localized security operations. It does not imply that all violence in coca-growing regions is part of one conflict. Event claims should be checked against official records, independent journalism and human-rights documentation.
