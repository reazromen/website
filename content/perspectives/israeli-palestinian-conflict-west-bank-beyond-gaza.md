# Israeli–Palestinian Armed Conflict Beyond Gaza: The West Bank and Other Theatres

The Israeli–Palestinian conflict is not confined to Gaza. The West Bank, including East Jerusalem, has experienced recurring military raids, armed attacks, arrests, settler violence, movement restrictions and lethal confrontations. These events differ in scale and organization from the war in Gaza, but they are connected to the same wider dispute over occupation, sovereignty, security, settlements and Palestinian political rights.

## Distinguishing actors and events

Israeli security forces conduct operations in the West Bank, often stating that they target armed groups or individuals suspected of attacks. Palestinian armed organizations and unaffiliated attackers have carried out shootings, stabbings and other attacks against Israeli soldiers and civilians. Settler violence against Palestinians and property is a separate category and should not be automatically attributed to the Israeli military unless evidence establishes involvement. Likewise, Palestinian political protest is not the same as armed activity.

The period after October 2023 saw intensified operations, restrictions and violence in several West Bank locations. Any specific claim about deaths, raids, arrests or displacement should be tied to a date, place and source. A regional label cannot establish the circumstances of each incident.

## Civilian impact

Palestinian communities have faced deaths and injuries, home demolitions, displacement, restrictions on movement and access to work, schools and medical care. Israeli communities have also experienced lethal attacks and fear of further violence. These harms should be recorded without implying that the parties have equal power, identical responsibilities or the same legal status. International humanitarian and human-rights law applies according to the relevant facts and legal framework.

## Relationship to Gaza and earlier conflict phases

The West Bank theatre intersects with the Gaza war but has distinct operational dynamics, actors and patterns of harm. The archive should maintain separate incident timelines and link them through the wider Israeli–Palestinian conflict. Earlier intifadas and recurring Gaza campaigns provide context but should not be treated as continuous, undifferentiated combat.

## Scope and sources

This entry focuses on organized armed violence and related lethal incidents outside Gaza, particularly in the West Bank and East Jerusalem. It does not classify every protest, arrest or political dispute as armed conflict. The archive will distinguish state force, armed-group attacks, settler violence and communal incidents where evidence supports those categories.
