# Somalia’s Conflict and the al-Shabaab Insurgency

Somalia’s armed conflicts since 2000 have involved the collapse and reconstruction of state institutions, competing political authorities, clan-based power arrangements and the insurgency led by al-Shabaab. These layers overlap but should not be collapsed into a single undifferentiated war. The actors, territorial reach and political context have changed repeatedly.

## State transition and insurgent consolidation

After the collapse of the central government in 1991, Somalia experienced years of fragmented authority and local warfare. Transitional institutions formed in the 2000s but faced armed opposition and limited control outside key areas. The Islamic Courts Union gained influence in 2006 before Ethiopian military intervention and the collapse of its governing structure. Al-Shabaab emerged as a durable insurgent organization from this period, later pledging allegiance to al-Qaeda.

Al-Shabaab has attacked Somali security forces, government institutions, African Union forces and civilians. It has also used assassinations, bombings, taxation and coercive control in areas where it operates. Government forces, regional administrations and international partners have conducted military operations against it, with front lines and territorial control changing over time.

## Federalism, clans and local conflict

Somalia’s federal system includes federal and member-state institutions whose relationships are sometimes contested. Clan dynamics can influence local alliances and disputes, but clan identity should not be treated as a fixed explanation for every event. Local conflicts over land, resources, political appointments and business interests may occur alongside the national insurgency and may require separate classification.

Al-Shabaab’s attacks in neighboring countries and cross-border operations have regional implications, especially for Kenya and Ethiopia. Those incidents should be recorded in their own national and operational contexts rather than automatically added to Somalia’s internal conflict totals.

## Civilian impact

Civilians face attacks, forced recruitment, extortion, displacement and restrictions on humanitarian access. Drought, flooding and food insecurity can magnify the consequences of violence, while conflict can prevent people from reaching assistance. Casualty figures and claims of territorial control need dates and source attribution, particularly when released by parties to the conflict.

## Archive method and sources

This entry tracks the broad Somalia conflict while linking al-Shabaab operations, regional interventions and localized disputes to narrower records. It distinguishes organized armed conflict from criminal violence and political tension.
