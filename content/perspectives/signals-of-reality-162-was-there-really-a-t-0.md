The phrase “13.8 billion years ago” sounds like a date. It is not quite the same kind of date as a calendar date. The age of the universe is inferred from a cosmological model that relates the present expansion state to an earlier history.

That distinction becomes important when we ask whether there was really a t = 0.

Modern cosmology has strong evidence for an expanding universe whose past was much hotter and denser than the universe around us. The cosmic microwave background, primordial element abundances, and the observed expansion fit together into a remarkably successful framework.

A coordinate marked zero is not, by itself, a demonstrated physical instant at which the universe began. It can be the endpoint of an extrapolation of a model.

When people say “the universe began,” they may mean a hot beginning of the observable cosmic history, a past boundary of classical spacetime, or an absolute metaphysical beginning. The evidence for the first is strong. The second occurs in important classical models. The third does not follow automatically from the first two.

A classical singularity is not an object sitting somewhere in space. In general relativity, singularity theorems concern properties of spacetime paths and can signal geodesic incompleteness. That is not equivalent to a tiny ball containing everything.

Inflation complicates the word beginning. It can explain important features of early-universe structure, but the Borde-Guth-Vilenkin theorem shows that sufficiently expanding inflationary spacetimes are past-incomplete under its assumptions. The result does not identify a unique physical beginning or prove that nothing existed before.

Some theories could replace the classical boundary with a transition into an earlier phase. Others could retain an initial boundary. If time is emergent, even the ordinary meaning of “earlier” may change.

The useful conclusion is disciplined: there is a t = 0 in some important mathematical descriptions of cosmic history. Whether nature has a literal first instant of time is an unresolved physical question.
