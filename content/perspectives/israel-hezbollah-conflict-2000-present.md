# Israel and Hezbollah: Border Conflict, the 2006 War, and Renewed Escalation

The conflict between Israel and Hezbollah spans Lebanon’s southern border, the occupied or disputed Shebaa Farms area, and wider regional theatres. Israel withdrew its forces from most of southern Lebanon in 2000, but disputes and armed incidents continued. Hezbollah, a Lebanese Shi’a political and armed organization supported by Iran, maintained a military role and presented itself as a resistance movement. Israel considers Hezbollah a major security threat and has conducted military operations against it in Lebanon and beyond.

## The 2006 war

The July 2006 war began after Hezbollah fighters crossed the border, killed Israeli soldiers and captured two. Israel launched a major air and ground campaign in Lebanon, while Hezbollah fired rockets into Israel. The month-long war caused extensive destruction in Lebanon, killed civilians and combatants on both sides, displaced large populations and damaged infrastructure. Israeli communities also experienced rocket attacks and displacement. UN Security Council Resolution 1701 called for a cessation of hostilities and arrangements intended to strengthen state authority and security along the border.

The ceasefire reduced large-scale fighting but did not resolve the underlying dispute over arms, border security, prisoners, sovereignty and the role of Hezbollah’s military wing. Both sides continued to prepare for future conflict, and periodic incidents sustained the risk of escalation.

## Renewed fighting after October 2023

After the Hamas-led attacks in Israel on 7 October 2023 and the ensuing Gaza war, Hezbollah and Israel exchanged fire across the Lebanon–Israel border. The escalation displaced civilians on both sides and expanded into a more intense campaign in 2024, including Israeli strikes and ground operations in Lebanon and Hezbollah rocket and missile attacks. The terms and implementation of ceasefire arrangements, and the security situation after them, require dated verification rather than assumptions that a ceasefire ended all hostilities.

This border theatre is connected to Gaza, Iran and other regional conflicts, but it is not identical to any of them. Claims about the presence of armed personnel, military objectives and civilian casualties should be evaluated event by event.

## Civilian impact and legal obligations

Residents have faced evacuation orders, damage to homes, agricultural land and infrastructure, disrupted schools and health services, and uncertainty about returning. Explosive remnants and damaged infrastructure can continue to pose risks after active fighting. All parties remain bound by international humanitarian law, including rules on distinction, proportionality and precautions. A claimed military objective does not remove the need to assess foreseeable civilian harm.

## Scope and sources

This article covers Israel–Hezbollah armed confrontation from 2000 onward, with 2006 and the post-2023 escalation treated as distinct phases. It cross-links the Gaza war and wider Israel–Iran confrontation without treating every linked event as one continuous conflict.
