# Chad’s Rebel Campaigns and Armed Transitions: Separate Episodes, Changing Coalitions

Chad’s armed history since 2000 consists of successive campaigns involving rebel coalitions, state forces and cross-border dynamics, rather than one unchanging war. Armed groups have operated from border areas and neighbouring countries, while domestic political struggles and regional conflicts influenced alliances. A coup or failed negotiation is not automatically an armed conflict; the archive must identify the organized fighting associated with each episode.

## Rebel coalitions and state response

During the 2000s, multiple rebel movements challenged President Idriss Déby’s government. Coalitions shifted, and some groups advanced toward N’Djamena, including during major fighting in 2006 and 2008. Sudan–Chad relations and the Darfur conflict affected rear bases, alliances and accusations of cross-border support. These links require evidence for each period rather than assumptions that neighbouring governments controlled every rebel operation.

Peace agreements and integration arrangements sometimes led to defections or new factions. A group’s name can persist even as its command changes, so records should track leadership and dates.

## The 2021 transition and Lake Chad

Déby’s death in 2021 during fighting with FACT led to a military transition. Later developments should be separated from earlier campaigns unless evidence shows continuity in the armed parties. Chad has also faced Boko Haram/ISWAP violence around Lake Chad, but that theatre is distinct from anti-government rebel campaigns.

## Classification and sources

Civilians have faced displacement, disrupted services and risks from clashes and counterinsurgency. Sources include UN Security Council and humanitarian reporting, International Crisis Group, UCDP and ACLED.
