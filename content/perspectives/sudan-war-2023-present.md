# Sudan’s War Since April 2023: The SAF, RSF, and Civilian Catastrophe

Sudan’s war began in April 2023 after a power struggle between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) erupted into open fighting. The conflict followed years of political upheaval after the removal of Omar al-Bashir and the breakdown of a transition toward civilian government. It is distinct from earlier wars in Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile, although the actors and patterns of violence are historically connected.

## From transition crisis to war

The SAF, led by Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, and the RSF, led by Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, had been partners in Sudan’s post-Bashir power structure. Disagreements over command, the integration of the RSF into the national military and control of the state escalated into fighting on 15 April 2023. Combat spread through Khartoum and other regions, with shifting front lines and attacks affecting urban neighborhoods, markets, hospitals and essential infrastructure.

The RSF’s origins in Darfur’s Janjaweed militia networks are central to understanding the conflict, but the 2023 war is not simply a continuation of the earlier Darfur war. Both SAF and RSF have been accused of serious abuses. Allegations should be described with careful attribution, while findings by credible investigations should be distinguished from claims made by the parties.

## Civilian harm and humanitarian access

The war has caused mass displacement inside Sudan and across its borders. Civilians have faced killings, sexual violence, looting, destruction of homes, interruption of healthcare and severe food insecurity. Humanitarian agencies have repeatedly warned that insecurity, bureaucratic restrictions and attacks on aid workers impede assistance. Figures change rapidly and vary by source; any numerical claim should carry a publication date and an explicit source.

Darfur has again seen severe violence, including ethnically targeted attacks reported in some areas. Those events should be cross-linked to the region’s longer history without erasing the nationwide SAF–RSF war. Other local conflicts may overlap with the war but require separate event-level evidence.

## Regional dimensions and accountability

External political and material support has influenced the conflict, though specific allegations require evidence and should not be presented as settled solely because a party makes them. The war has destabilized neighboring countries through refugee flows, trade disruption and cross-border insecurity.

International humanitarian law requires parties to distinguish civilians from combatants, protect medical services and allow impartial humanitarian relief. Allegations of war crimes and crimes against humanity require credible investigation and due process. A political settlement must address civilian governance, security-sector reform, accountability and the needs of displaced communities.
