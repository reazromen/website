# The Tigray War in Ethiopia, 2020–2022

The Tigray war began in November 2020 after a prolonged political and security crisis between Ethiopia’s federal government and the Tigray regional leadership escalated into open fighting. The conflict involved the Ethiopian National Defence Force, Tigrayan forces, Amhara regional forces and Eritrean forces, whose participation has been documented by investigators and disputed or described differently by parties at various points. The war caused mass displacement, widespread destruction and severe restrictions on humanitarian access.

## Escalation and changing front lines

Relations between the federal government and Tigray’s ruling Tigray People’s Liberation Front (TPLF) deteriorated amid disputes over elections, constitutional authority and security command. Fighting spread rapidly after November 2020. The federal government announced a military operation, while Tigrayan forces continued resistance after losing control of Mekelle. The conflict expanded and shifted through 2021, with Tigrayan forces advancing beyond Tigray before federal and allied forces regained territory.

The war was not confined to two neatly bounded armies. Regional forces, allied militias and external actors affected the battlefield, while the status and command of different units varied over time. Specific allegations about responsibility for attacks should be attributed to credible investigations and not inferred solely from ethnic identity or political alignment.

## Civilian harm and humanitarian restrictions

Reports documented killings, sexual violence, arbitrary detention, forced displacement and destruction of civilian infrastructure. Communication blackouts and access restrictions complicated independent verification. Severe food insecurity and disruptions to health services compounded the consequences. Estimates of deaths vary widely; figures should be quoted only with source, date and clear explanation of uncertainty.

## Cessation agreement

In November 2022, the federal government and TPLF signed the Pretoria Cessation of Hostilities Agreement, followed by related implementation arrangements. The agreement sharply reduced large-scale fighting, but disarmament, restoration of services, return of displaced people and accountability remained complex. A peace agreement is an important status marker, not proof that all consequences or local security risks have ended.

## Archive classification

The Tigray war is a distinct state-based armed conflict, linked to but separate from the Amhara and Oromia conflicts. The archive should track the 2020–2022 war and later incidents according to evidence and applicable conflict thresholds.
