# Kashmir’s Insurgency and India–Pakistan Armed Crises: Separate Conflicts, Shared Risks

Kashmir is not one simple conflict with one set of parties. It is a disputed region divided by a Line of Control, claimed in different ways by India and Pakistan, and shaped by the political aspirations of people who live there. Since the late 1980s, an armed insurgency in Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir has involved local militants, organizations with cross-border links, Indian security forces and a wider political movement whose participants do not all share the same goals. The territorial dispute between India and Pakistan is related to this insurgency but is not identical to it.

## The insurgency and the state

The insurgency grew amid political alienation, disputed electoral legitimacy, demands for self-determination and wider India–Pakistan rivalry. Armed groups have pursued different objectives, including independence, accession to Pakistan or Islamist political goals. Their tactics have included attacks on security forces, assassinations and attacks that killed civilians. Indian security operations have included counterinsurgency campaigns, arrests, curfews and restrictions on movement; human-rights groups have documented allegations of abuses and impunity. The experiences of civilians vary by place and period, and the archive should not treat Kashmiris as a single political constituency.

## Crises between two nuclear-armed states

The 2001 attack on India’s parliament and the 2001–2002 military standoff brought Indian and Pakistani forces into a prolonged high-alert confrontation. The crisis showed how an attack by a non-state armed group could trigger mobilization by two nuclear-armed states. In 2016, an attack on an Indian military installation at Uri was followed by India’s announced cross-Line-of-Control “surgical strikes,” whose details and interpretation were disputed. In February 2019, a suicide bombing at Pulwama killed 40 Indian paramilitary personnel; Jaish-e-Mohammed claimed responsibility. India then carried out an airstrike inside Pakistan, and Pakistan responded with its own air operation and the capture and later release of an Indian pilot.

These episodes should be dated separately. They involved different immediate triggers, military actions and diplomatic outcomes. They should not be merged into one uninterrupted interstate war, nor should they be reduced to the insurgency alone. The Line of Control has seen recurring exchanges of fire, while ceasefire understandings have periodically reduced violence without resolving the underlying dispute.

## Political changes and contested futures

In August 2019, India revoked Jammu and Kashmir’s special constitutional status and reorganized the former state into union territories. The decision changed the region’s constitutional arrangement and was accompanied by communications restrictions and detentions. India described the changes as integration and governance reform; critics raised concerns about political representation, civil liberties and the absence of local consent. These developments form part of the political context but are not, by themselves, proof of an armed-conflict event.

The region’s future remains linked to security, political representation, civil liberties, cross-border relations and the wishes of its diverse communities. Official positions from India and Pakistan differ sharply, while Kashmiri political views include a range of positions that should not be collapsed into either state’s narrative.

## Civilian impact and evidence

Civilians have experienced killings, injuries, displacement, disrupted livelihoods, restrictions on movement and prolonged uncertainty. Casualty counts differ according to whether they include militants, security personnel, civilians, missing persons or particular districts and time periods. Any responsible summary should state the period and definition rather than present a single figure as definitive. Claims about responsibility for attacks or cross-border support should be attributed to the source making them and compared with independent reporting.

## Classification for this archive

This entry is a linked overview of two related layers: the insurgency in Indian-administered Kashmir and dated interstate crises involving India and Pakistan. The archive will link specific episodes where the evidence supports separate records. This approach avoids double counting and keeps the local conflict distinct from state-to-state escalation.
