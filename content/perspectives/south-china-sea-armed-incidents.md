# South China Sea Armed Incidents and Maritime Confrontations

The South China Sea is a strategic maritime region where China and several Southeast Asian states have overlapping claims, fishing and energy interests, and competing interpretations of maritime rights. Confrontations have involved coast guard vessels, maritime militia, fishing fleets, naval forces and civilian ships. These incidents can carry serious escalation risks, but they do not all constitute armed conflict.

## Confrontation below the threshold of war

Many incidents involve water cannons, blocking manoeuvres, collisions, ramming, lasers, dangerous intercepts or detention. Such acts can endanger crews and raise diplomatic tensions without involving lethal force. Other events may include injuries or damage and require closer assessment. The archive should describe the actual conduct rather than use “battle” or “war” as a generic label for every maritime confrontation.

The disputes also differ by location. China’s claims overlap with those of the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Brunei and Taiwan in different ways. The 2016 arbitral award in the Philippines–China case addressed legal questions under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, but it did not remove all political and operational disputes. Claims, legal findings and on-water incidents should be recorded separately.

## Why the distinction matters

Military deployments, coast guard patrols and legal disputes are important context, but they are not the same as organized lethal violence. The archive should use dated incident reports, identify the vessels and agencies involved, and record whether casualties or physical damage occurred. Attribution can be disputed, especially immediately after an incident.

## Archive treatment

This page is a maritime security and incident index. It does not assert a single continuous South China Sea war. If a discrete lethal clash meets the chosen criteria, it can be documented as a dated event; otherwise it should remain a confrontation record linked to the broader maritime dispute.
