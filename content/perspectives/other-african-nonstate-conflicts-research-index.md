# Other Documented African Non-State Conflicts: A Research Index

“Other African non-state conflicts” is a research index, not the name of a single war. Across the continent, organized armed violence has occurred between non-state groups, including communal militias, pastoralist formations, political factions and other armed organizations. These events can be severe without fitting the familiar model of a government fighting a rebellion.

## What the category captures

UCDP’s non-state conflict data concerns organized violence between non-state actors that meets its coding rules. It is distinct from state-based conflict, where a government is a party, and from one-sided violence against civilians. Those categories may occur in the same location and period, but they should not be merged into one count.

Examples of regions requiring careful review include parts of the Sahel, the Horn of Africa, the Great Lakes region, Nigeria’s Middle Belt and areas affected by communal or militia competition. This list is not an assertion that every local dispute meets the dataset threshold. The identity of the parties, the number of fatalities, the period of activity and the reliability of reporting must be checked case by case.

## Why labels matter

A broad geographic category can conceal distinct causes. One episode may involve competition over land or grazing, another political mobilization, and another armed groups connected to a larger insurgency. Similar labels such as “communal violence” or “militia clashes” are not sufficient by themselves to establish continuity between events. Nor should every criminal killing be classified as armed conflict.

## How the archive will use this page

This entry points researchers toward separate, documented cases. Where a recurring campaign has identifiable parties and a defensible timeline, it should receive a dedicated article. Smaller or episodic events may be better represented in a sourced chronology rather than promoted into a standalone war. Overlapping country-year records are not unique-conflict counts.

The goal is transparent coverage, not an inflated number of wars. Any final list should preserve the distinction between dataset discovery, editorial interpretation and a verified conflict classification.
