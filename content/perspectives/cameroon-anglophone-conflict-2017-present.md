# Cameroon’s Anglophone Conflict: Separatist Groups and State Forces

Cameroon’s Anglophone conflict grew from long-running grievances in the English-speaking Northwest and Southwest regions. Disputes over language, education, legal institutions and political representation escalated in 2016, when lawyers’ and teachers’ protests met a forceful state response. Some activists subsequently pursued secession through armed struggle, and sustained fighting expanded from 2017.

## From protest to armed conflict

The separatist project commonly called Ambazonia includes multiple armed groups and political organizations, not one unified command. Armed separatists have attacked security forces and civilians, including through kidnappings and movement restrictions. Government forces have conducted security operations and have also faced credible allegations of unlawful killings, arbitrary detention and destruction of homes. Claims should be attributed to specific incidents and investigations.

School boycotts and attacks on education have harmed children and interrupted learning. Communities have faced displacement, disrupted health services, farming and trade, and pressure from competing actors. Civilians may be coerced by more than one side.

## Fragmented leadership and peace efforts

Separatist factions differ in leadership, strategy and territorial presence. A statement from one group should not automatically be attributed to all factions. Dialogue initiatives have faced obstacles including fragmented command, distrust and disagreement over the regions’ political future.

The conflict is distinct from Boko Haram/ISWAP violence in Cameroon’s Far North.

## Classification and sources

This entry follows organized fighting in the Anglophone regions, distinguishing it from earlier protest, peaceful advocacy and unrelated criminal incidents. Sources include UN human-rights and humanitarian reporting, International Crisis Group, UCDP and ACLED.

Archive note: Do not treat Anglophone civilians as synonymous with separatist armed groups.
