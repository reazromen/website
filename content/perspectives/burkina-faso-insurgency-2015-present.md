# Burkina Faso’s Insurgency: Armed Groups, State Forces, and Civilian Defence

Burkina Faso’s armed conflict intensified from 2015, first around the northern borders with Mali and Niger and later across several regions. Armed groups linked to al-Qaeda and the Islamic State have fought state forces and attacked civilians. The government has also mobilized local civilian auxiliaries, whose roles and conduct must be distinguished from those of regular military units.

## From border insecurity to national conflict

The conflict grew alongside the wider Sahel insurgency. Armed groups exploited cross-border routes, weak state presence and local grievances. Attacks on security posts and transport routes were followed by raids on villages and public services. Violence displaced large numbers of people and disrupted farming, markets and education.

Jama’at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM), Islamic State Sahel Province and other local formations should be distinguished when incident-specific reporting supports attribution. A claim of responsibility is not independent confirmation.

## State response and civilian protection

Successive governments expanded military operations and relied on Volunteers for the Defence of the Homeland (VDP). Such auxiliaries may provide local knowledge, but recruitment and deployment raise questions about command, accountability and reprisals against communities. Human-rights allegations against armed groups and state-aligned actors require careful attribution.

Military coups in 2022 changed the political leadership and security partnerships but did not themselves end the conflict. Claims about territory or casualty totals should be dated and independently checked.

## Classification and sources

This entry tracks organized fighting involving state forces and insurgent organizations. It links to Mali and Niger but should not double-count their incidents. Sources for verification include UN humanitarian and human-rights reporting, International Crisis Group, UCDP and ACLED.

Archive note: Do not attribute every violent incident to jihadist groups or equate civilian auxiliaries with regular state forces without evidence.
