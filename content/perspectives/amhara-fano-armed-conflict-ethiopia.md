# The Amhara Armed Conflict: Fano, Federal Forces, and Ethiopia’s Security Crisis

The Amhara armed conflict escalated in 2023 when tensions over the reorganization of regional security forces and the integration of local forces into federal structures triggered widespread clashes. Fano is not a single centralized organization with one universally accepted command; the label refers to a range of Amhara militia and mobilization networks whose leadership and aims can vary by place and period. The archive should preserve those distinctions.

## Security reorganization and escalation

After the Tigray war, the Ethiopian government pursued changes to regional security arrangements. In Amhara, the proposed integration or dissolution of regional special forces generated resistance among armed networks and local constituencies. Fighting intensified between federal forces and Fano groups, affecting towns, roads and rural areas. The conflict has also been shaped by political grievances, disputes over territory and the aftermath of the Tigray war.

Because Fano networks are decentralized, a statement from one faction cannot automatically be treated as the position of all groups. Likewise, a reported clash involving one local unit should not be generalized to every Amhara community. Claims about attacks, arrests and territorial control need dated, location-specific sourcing.

## Civilian consequences

Residents have faced displacement, disruption to trade and education, restrictions on movement and risks from fighting in populated areas. Human-rights organizations and independent monitors have raised concerns about abuses by different actors. Emergency measures and communication restrictions can make independent verification more difficult. Casualty estimates should be attributed and not presented as settled when sources conflict.

## Relationship to other Ethiopian conflicts

The Amhara conflict followed the Tigray war and exists alongside the Oromo Liberation Army conflict in Oromia and other regional security challenges. These theatres can interact through political alliances, military deployments and population movement, but they are not one war. The archive should cross-link them without merging actor lists or timelines.

## Classification and sources

This entry follows organized fighting between Fano-linked forces and federal or allied forces, while keeping localized communal violence and criminal incidents separate unless evidence establishes an armed-conflict connection.
