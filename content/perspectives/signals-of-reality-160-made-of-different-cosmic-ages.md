How old are you?

Biologically, the answer is straightforward enough.

How old is the matter in you?

Now the question fragments.

The hydrogen.

Carbon.

Oxygen.

Nitrogen.

Calcium.

Iron.

Trace elements.

They do not share one cosmic birthday.

We are assembled from different ages of the universe.

## Hydrogen carries primordial ancestry

Much of the hydrogen in the cosmos originates in the early universe.

Its nuclei formed before stars.

The hydrogen in water and organic molecules therefore belongs to the oldest common element population available to later chemistry.

That does not mean each atom remained in one molecule for billions of years.

Chemical identity changes constantly.

Nuclear identity can persist across many molecular lives.

## Carbon and oxygen needed stars

Heavier elements came later.

Stars fused nuclei under high temperature and pressure.

Different stellar masses and evolutionary stages produced different elements.

Explosions and winds returned them to space.

Carbon in cells and oxygen in water therefore required generations of astrophysical processing absent from the primordial universe.

Their existence is evidence that the universe had already lived through stars.

## Iron has another history

Iron-group elements are produced through stellar and explosive nucleosynthesis.

The details are more complex than the slogan "iron comes from supernovae," because multiple astrophysical channels contribute.

The important point is chronology.

Iron-rich rocky planets could not exist before the universe developed sources capable of making and distributing such nuclei.

Planet composition remembers earlier stellar evolution.

## Heavier elements add still more sources

Elements heavier than iron are created significantly through neutron-capture processes.

Some form in evolved stars.

Some form in violent events such as neutron-star mergers and certain stellar explosions.

The periodic table in a body is therefore not one stellar recipe.

It is a mixed archive of astrophysical environments.

Our chemistry is cosmically plural.

## The Solar System mixed those histories

The molecular cloud that formed the Sun contained primordial material plus matter processed by previous stars.

Within the protoplanetary disk, elements condensed, reacted, separated, and formed solids.

Earth inherited one local sample of galactic chemical evolution.

Geology then remixed it.

Biology remixed it again.

A calcium atom in bone passed through several system boundaries before becoming biological.

## Age depends on what object we mean

The nucleus may be ancient.

The atom's electron configuration changes continually.

The molecule may be recent.

The cell may be years old.

The organism has another age.

Identity is layered.

Ask the age of "this water" and we need to clarify:

this molecule?

these atoms?

these nuclei?

this ocean reservoir?

Different levels have different histories.

## "Star stuff" needs an asterisk

The phrase is beautiful because many elements essential to life were built by stars.

But not all matter in us came from stellar interiors.

Hydrogen is the obvious exception.

Some nuclei trace directly to primordial nucleosynthesis.

A more complete statement is:

we are made of early-universe matter and stellar-processed matter organized by planetary and biological history.

Less poetic.

More interesting.

## A body is a temporary assembly

The atoms in us existed before the organism and will continue after it.

Biology creates a pattern from borrowed matter.

Metabolism constantly replaces some of that matter while preserving organization.

We are made of different ages of the universe.

The unity of a body is therefore not unity of origin.

It is a present structure assembled from matter whose histories began at radically different times.
