Calling mutation a **random code change** is useful for about five minutes. The comparison can expose structure that ordinary language hides. The problem begins when the comparison stops behaving like a tool and starts behaving like a claim about what the thing literally is.

The code-change analogy highlights persistence, copying errors, repair and robustness. It also reminds engineers that a system's future depends partly on how variation is introduced and tolerated.

## What the metaphor gets right

The code-change analogy highlights persistence, copying errors, repair and robustness. It also reminds engineers that a system's future depends partly on how variation is introduced and tolerated.

This is why engineering metaphors are so attractive. They compress a complicated relationship into something we can manipulate mentally. In the best case, the metaphor produces predictions and sharper questions.

## What it smuggles in

Software bugs violate an intended specification. Evolution has no fixed specification, and mutation is not merely damage; variation is also raw material for population change.

Engineering words often carry hidden assumptions: a designer, a specification, modular ownership, explicit goals, a stable machine underneath the program. Those assumptions must be tested rather than inherited.

## Return to the physical story

Mutations are changes in genetic material produced by physical and biochemical processes. They are not generated because an organism needs a particular adaptation, and their effects depend on genomic position, background and environment.

Once the mechanism is back in view, the analogy can be kept on a short leash. We can state which relation is genuinely similar and which difference would make the analogy generate a wrong prediction.

## My three tests for a metaphor

1. **What mechanism does this comparison help me see?**
2. **Which engineering assumption does the natural system not share?**
3. **What would I predict incorrectly if I took the comparison literally?**

The third test is the most important. An analogy that cannot fail is decoration, not explanation.

## Beyond the borrowed language

Mutation became central to evolutionary theory when genetics was integrated with natural selection in the modern synthesis.

Mutation makes contingency unavoidable: every lineage carries a history of events that did not have to happen exactly that way.

How much identity depends on preserving information, and how much depends on allowing it to change?

A good metaphor should eventually make itself less necessary. It gets us close enough to the real system that we can see where the scaffolding ends.
