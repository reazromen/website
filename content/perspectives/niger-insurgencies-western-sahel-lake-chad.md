# Niger’s Insurgencies: Western Sahel and Lake Chad Theatres

Niger’s armed violence since the mid-2010s has involved two geographically distinct theatres. In the west, near Mali and Burkina Faso, armed groups linked to the wider Sahel insurgency have attacked security forces and civilians. In the southeast, Diffa and the Lake Chad region have been affected by Boko Haram and Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP). Regional connections matter, but the theatres should not be merged into one campaign.

## Western Niger

Tillabéri and Tahoua have experienced attacks and raids involving groups operating across the Mali–Niger–Burkina Faso borderlands. Jama’at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin and Islamic State Sahel Province are distinct organizations; attribution should rely on evidence rather than geography alone. Local disputes and weak public services provide context but do not explain every incident.

## Lake Chad and political transition

Diffa has faced cross-border violence associated with Boko Haram and ISWAP. Attacks, abductions and military operations have disrupted farming, fishing, trade and movement. These groups have distinct leadership and strategies, so the names should not be used interchangeably where reporting identifies a faction.

Niger’s 2023 coup changed foreign security relationships, but political transition alone does not establish that insurgencies ended or that territory is securely controlled.

## Classification and sources

This national overview links western Sahel violence and the Lake Chad insurgency while cross-referencing Burkina Faso, Mali and the regional Boko Haram record. Sources include UN humanitarian reporting, UN Security Council reports, International Crisis Group, UCDP and ACLED.
