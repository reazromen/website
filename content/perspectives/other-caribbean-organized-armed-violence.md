# Other Caribbean Organized Armed Violence: Scope and Classification

The Caribbean is not one conflict theatre. Countries and territories across the region face different combinations of gang violence, firearms trafficking, political intimidation, organized crime and state security responses. Some episodes are severe and sustained, but the region should not be classified as one armed conflict merely because multiple countries face security challenges.

## Different contexts

Haiti’s gang warfare has developed into a major crisis involving territorial control, attacks on civilians and pressure on state institutions. Jamaica has experienced localized gang violence and state security operations. Trinidad and Tobago and other Caribbean states face their own patterns of organized crime, firearms use and community-level violence. These cases differ in actors, political context, levels of organization and the relationship between violence and state institutions.

A regional label can be useful for studying firearms flows, trafficking routes or policy cooperation. It is less useful as a conflict label unless it identifies a defined cross-border armed organization and a documented campaign. Similar tactics do not prove that groups in different countries share command or belong to one war.

## Evidence and definitions

Crime statistics, homicide rates, human-rights reporting and armed-conflict datasets measure different phenomena. A homicide increase may be alarming without establishing an organized armed conflict. Conversely, a localized campaign may require attention even if it is small relative to national crime totals. The source and definition behind every number should be stated.

## Archive treatment

This page is a research index for country-specific cases outside the dedicated Haiti entry. It does not assert a single Caribbean war. Each potential standalone article should establish named actors, dates, organized lethal violence and the scope of state involvement. If those criteria are not met, the case should remain a public-security context entry rather than be counted as an armed conflict.
