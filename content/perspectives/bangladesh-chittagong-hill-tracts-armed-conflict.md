# Bangladesh’s Chittagong Hill Tracts: Armed Conflict, Accord and Residual Violence

The Chittagong Hill Tracts (CHT) in southeastern Bangladesh have a distinct history of Indigenous political mobilization, state security policy, displacement and armed conflict. The 1997 Chittagong Hill Tracts Accord was a major political milestone, but implementation disputes and competition among local organizations continued after it. The post-2000 record therefore needs a careful distinction between the historical insurgency, local armed incidents and broader political grievances.

## After the 1997 accord

The accord addressed regional autonomy, land disputes, rehabilitation and institutional arrangements. Its implementation has remained contested. Different organizations have represented competing political strategies and constituencies, and factions have split over the pace and terms of settlement. Reports of violence can involve armed groups, local rivalries, security forces or criminal actors; they should not all be attributed to one continuous insurgency without evidence.

## What counts as armed conflict?

A region may experience intimidation, extortion, abduction, political violence and isolated lethal incidents without every episode meeting the same armed-conflict definition. Reliable classification requires named actors, organized conduct, a defensible period and evidence for lethal violence. The archive should not equate the presence of weapons or the persistence of political grievances with continuous warfare.

## Civilian impact and sources

Residents may face threats to safety, land tenure, political participation and access to services. Different organizations and state bodies may describe the same incident differently. The record should attribute allegations, state what is independently established and avoid generalizing from one district to the entire CHT.

## Archive treatment

This page documents the CHT as a post-accord security and political context with possible actor-specific armed episodes. It does not assert that the whole region has experienced one uninterrupted post-2000 war. Future updates should identify dated incidents, parties and reliable evidence before changing the classification.
