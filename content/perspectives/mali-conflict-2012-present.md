# Mali’s Conflict Since 2012: Rebellion, Jihadist Insurgency, and Military Rule

Mali’s conflict escalated in 2012 when a Tuareg-led rebellion in the north coincided with a military coup and the expansion of jihadist armed groups. Since then, the conflict has evolved through shifting alliances, foreign interventions, peace agreements and political upheaval. It is not a single confrontation between the Malian state and one unified opponent.

## The 2012 crisis

The National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad (MNLA) launched a rebellion seeking independence or autonomy for northern Mali. A coup in Bamako weakened the state’s response, while jihadist groups including Ansar Dine, al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb and the Movement for Oneness and Jihad in West Africa gained influence in northern towns. The jihadist groups later displaced or marginalized some separatist forces and imposed harsh rule in areas under their control.

French forces intervened in 2013 at the request of Mali’s authorities, alongside regional and international deployments. The intervention reversed some territorial gains but did not end insurgent violence. Armed groups expanded attacks into central Mali and neighboring Burkina Faso and Niger, creating a wider Sahel security crisis.

## Peace efforts and changing external roles

The 2015 Algiers peace agreement sought to address northern grievances and integrate some former combatants, but implementation remained incomplete. Mali experienced coups in 2020 and 2021, followed by a reorientation of security partnerships. France’s military presence ended, and the UN peacekeeping mission MINUSMA withdrew in 2023 at the request of Mali’s transitional authorities. These changes altered the security landscape but did not themselves resolve the conflict.

Jihadist organizations, separatist groups, community self-defence forces and state-aligned units have pursued different objectives. Some local conflicts concern land, grazing, political representation and communal security, and should not automatically be labelled jihadist operations. Allegations of abuses by all sides require independent, dated sourcing.

## Civilian impact and accountability

Civilians have faced attacks, displacement, restrictions on movement, disrupted markets and school closures. Reports of executions and other abuses require careful attribution, especially where access is limited and parties issue competing accounts. Counterterrorism operations must comply with international humanitarian and human-rights law.
