Biology clearly contains information.

DNA sequence is inherited.

Cells send signals.

Neurons encode relationships to stimuli.

Immune systems distinguish molecular patterns.

Development depends on regulated gene expression.

So why resist the software metaphor?

Because information is broader than software.

We can use information theory and computational ideas without pretending cells are laptops.

## Information describes distinctions

At its most general, information concerns distinguishable alternatives.

Which nucleotide?

Which receptor state?

Which neural pattern?

Which developmental signal?

Which allele?

Biology is full of systems that preserve, transform, and respond to distinctions.

That makes information language legitimate.

The mistake is assuming that every information-processing system must share computer architecture.

## DNA is a physical polymer

DNA is not an abstract string stored outside chemistry.

Its bases have molecular structure.

Sequence affects binding.

DNA bends.

It is packaged.

It can be methylated.

It breaks and is repaired.

It exists inside chromatin and cells.

The symbolic representation A, C, G, T is extraordinarily useful because it captures one important dimension.

It deliberately ignores others.

The string is a map of a molecule.

## The "reader" is part of biology

Software source is interpreted by machinery designed separately.

DNA is read by proteins and RNA molecules that are themselves produced within living systems.

Regulatory networks alter which sequences are accessible.

Cell state changes interpretation.

The reader is not fixed.

Evolution modifies both the sequence and the machinery that interprets it.

This feedback has no simple equivalent in ordinary software.

## Signals have energetic cost

A cell cannot process unlimited information for free.

Receptors must be made.

Ion gradients maintained.

Proteins synthesized.

Molecules transported.

Errors corrected.

Biological information is constrained by thermodynamics.

A signal channel that consumes too much energy can reduce fitness.

The organism has an information budget embodied in metabolism.

## Noise is part of the computation

Gene expression fluctuates.

Molecular numbers can be small.

Binding is probabilistic.

Development still becomes remarkably reliable.

Biological systems often exploit feedback, redundancy, thresholds, and population-level averaging to operate under noise.

Reliability emerges from dynamics rather than perfect deterministic execution.

This is a useful meeting point between information theory and biology.

## Evolution changes the codebook

In engineered communications, protocol designers define symbol meanings.

In biology, signaling relationships evolve.

A receptor mutates.

A ligand changes.

Regulation shifts.

Networks are co-opted.

The mapping between signal and response is historically contingent.

There is no eternal specification document.

The codebook is part of the evolving system.

## Computation can be a model without being an identity

We can model gene networks as logic.

Neurons as dynamical systems.

Evolution as search.

Metabolism as networks.

These abstractions reveal structure.

They become misleading only when the model is mistaken for ontology.

A cell may be computable in many useful ways without literally being a computer in the everyday engineering sense.

## Keep the insight, drop the reduction

Information language helps connect biology to physics, mathematics, and engineering.

It lets us ask quantitative questions about transmission, uncertainty, fidelity, and regulation.

Software language contributes ideas about modularity, feedback, error correction, and state.

Use them.

Then remember the body.

Biology is information embodied in chemistry, energy flow, geometry, history, reproduction, and environment.

We do not need to call life software to appreciate that it processes signals.

The richer view is that information is one dimension of living matter—not a replacement for living matter.
