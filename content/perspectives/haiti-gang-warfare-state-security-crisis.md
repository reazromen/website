# Haiti’s Gang Warfare and the State-Security Crisis

Haiti’s security crisis has intensified as armed gangs expanded territorial control, formed and dissolved coalitions, attacked civilians and challenged already weakened state institutions. The crisis is shaped by political instability, economic hardship, impunity and the limited reach of public security services. Yet “gang warfare,” “organized criminal violence” and “armed conflict” are not interchangeable labels.

## Armed groups and territorial control

Gang organizations have exercised coercive control in parts of metropolitan Port-au-Prince and other areas. Alliances can change, and a coalition name may cover groups with separate leaders and local interests. Attacks on police, roadblocks, kidnappings, killings and forced displacement have had severe effects on daily life. Claims about who planned an attack or controlled a neighbourhood should be tied to specific reporting and dates.

The Haitian National Police has conducted operations against armed groups, while international support and a multinational security mission have sought to strengthen security capacity. These actions should be recorded separately from gang-on-gang fighting and one-sided attacks on civilians. The presence of a foreign security mission does not itself prove that the country is in an international armed conflict.

## Classification matters

UCDP’s definitions depend on organized actors and thresholds for lethal violence. Other monitoring organizations may use different methods to track gang violence, homicide or human-rights abuses. Those data can all be valuable, but they answer different questions. A high homicide count alone does not establish a single civil war, and a legal or political description should not be inferred from a dataset label.

## Archive treatment

This article treats Haiti as a complex organized-violence theatre requiring incident-level and actor-level documentation. It distinguishes gang conflict, state operations and one-sided violence and avoids presenting every criminal act as part of one unified war. The record should state its source, definition and period whenever it gives casualty or incident totals.
