# Haiti’s Gang Warfare and State-Security Crisis

Haiti’s security crisis has involved armed gangs, political networks, police operations, attacks on civilians and the erosion of public institutions. Gang coalitions have controlled roads and neighbourhoods, disrupted access to ports and essential services, and used kidnapping and sexual violence. The crisis intensified sharply after the 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moïse and subsequent institutional breakdown.

## Timeline and actors

The term “gang warfare” describes organized armed violence but does not by itself settle how each episode should be classified in a conflict dataset. Analysts must distinguish gang-on-gang fighting, gang attacks on state forces, state use of force and one-sided violence against civilians. International security missions and sanctions form part of the response, but do not replace Haitian political solutions or accountable institutions.

## Scope and classification

This entry tracks organized violence and civilian protection rather than treating all crime or political instability as war. Counts should identify time period, actor, incident type and source.
