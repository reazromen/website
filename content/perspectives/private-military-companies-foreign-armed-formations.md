# Private Military Companies and Foreign Armed Formations

Private military companies (PMCs), state-linked security contractors and foreign armed formations have played roles in several conflicts since 2000. The categories are not interchangeable. A commercial security company may guard facilities or train personnel; a state-linked formation may conduct frontline combat; and a foreign volunteer unit may fight alongside a national army. Legal status, command arrangements and the evidence of direct participation vary by case.

## Why the label can mislead

The term “PMC” is sometimes used politically for forces whose ownership, funding or command relationship is disputed. A group may be formally private while operating in close coordination with a state. Conversely, a foreign volunteer unit can have a distinct command structure despite fighting on behalf of a government. The archive should not infer the chain of command solely from a name or official accusation.

Examples of external armed formations have appeared in Syria, Libya, the Central African Republic, Mali, Ukraine and other theatres. Their roles and status changed over time. A report about recruitment, training or security contracts does not by itself prove direct combat participation. Evidence should specify the organization, deployment period, activity and source.

## Accountability and civilian impact

When armed formations operate across borders, questions arise about applicable law, accountability, detention, civilian harm and responsibility for operations. Claims of abuse should be attributed to credible investigations, court records, UN reporting or carefully sourced journalism. The archive should distinguish confirmed findings from allegations and should not treat every contractor as a combatant.

## Archive treatment

This page is an actor and intervention cross-index. Each deployment should be linked to the primary conflict and assessed separately. It should not create a single “PMC war” across unrelated countries, nor count the same battle again merely because an external formation participated.
