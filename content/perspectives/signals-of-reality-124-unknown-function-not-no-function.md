Delete a sequence in cultured cells.

Nothing obvious happens.

Has science proved that the sequence has no function?

No.

It has shown that under those tested conditions, with those assays, no relevant effect was detected.

That can be valuable evidence.

It is not the same claim.

Unknown function and no function are different categories.

## Experiments create windows

A biological function can depend on:

cell type,

developmental stage,

age,

temperature,

nutrition,

infection,

stress,

competition,

or environment.

An assay examines some subset of this space.

A mouse in a controlled laboratory does not experience every ecological condition its lineage evolved under.

A cell line is not an entire organism.

A short experiment may miss effects that emerge over generations.

The experimental window constrains the conclusion.

## Redundancy can mask contribution

Knock out one gene and nothing changes.

Maybe it was unnecessary.

Maybe another gene compensates.

Biological networks often contain overlapping functions.

A single perturbation can underestimate contribution because the system adapts.

Double knockouts, sensitized backgrounds, environmental challenges, and quantitative phenotyping can reveal effects hidden in simple tests.

"No phenotype" depends on how hard we looked.

## Small effects can matter evolutionarily

A sequence may alter reproductive success by a fraction too small for one laboratory experiment to detect easily.

Over many generations, small fitness differences can matter.

Conversely, measurable molecular effects can be too small to matter evolutionarily.

The scale of observation matters.

Biochemical function, organismal phenotype, and evolutionary constraint are different questions.

## Conservation can provide another clue

If a sequence remains strongly conserved across long evolutionary periods, that can suggest purifying selection.

It is not automatic proof of one specific function.

But it tells us substitutions may be costly.

Combining comparative genomics with experiments can therefore reveal function that either method alone would miss.

Independent evidence is powerful.

## Unknown is scientifically productive

An unknown-function gene or sequence can be studied systematically.

Where is it expressed?

What binds it?

What happens under stress?

Which species conserve it?

Which pathways change after perturbation?

Does the phenotype appear only in one tissue?

Unknown becomes a research program.

The category preserves curiosity without inventing a story.

## Negative evidence can become strong

We should not swing too far.

After many sensitive experiments across relevant contexts, weak evolutionary constraint, and no reproducible effect, confidence that a sequence lacks an important function can increase.

Science can support absence claims.

It just needs an experiment capable of seeing the thing whose absence is claimed.

That is the same logic as any non-detection.

## Language should preserve the boundary

"Non-functional" is stronger than "no function detected."

"Unknown function" is stronger than "probably important."

Careful wording prevents data from carrying more certainty than the experiment earned.

Unknown function ≠ no function.

The symbol is useful because it forces one small discipline:

do not turn a gap in current knowledge into a property of biology itself.
