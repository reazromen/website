# Drone and Remote-Strike Campaigns, 2000–2026

Drones and other remote-strike systems have become increasingly important in military operations since 2000. States and non-state actors have used remotely piloted aircraft for surveillance, targeting, attack and propaganda. The term “drone campaign” can describe a pattern of operations, but it is a method-of-warfare category rather than a conflict classification by itself.

## Different operational contexts

US drone strikes in Pakistan, Yemen and Somalia have been associated with counterterrorism operations and local conflicts. In Libya, Syria, Iraq and Ukraine, unmanned systems have served different tactical and strategic roles. Armed groups have also used commercially available drones or adapted systems. These cases differ in target selection, command structure, legal authority and the reliability of public casualty information.

A strike may be part of an existing armed conflict, a counterterrorism operation outside a recognized battlefield, or an incident whose legal classification is contested. The fact that a drone was used does not settle whether the action was lawful, whether a person was a combatant, or which conflict dataset should include the event.

## Civilian harm and accountability

Casualty counts can differ because governments, local authorities, journalists and monitoring organizations use different methods and have unequal access. An archive should identify the source, date, location, claimed target and any available investigation. It should separate confirmed deaths from estimates and allegations. Where attribution is uncertain, the record should say so.

## Archive treatment

This page is a cross-conflict index. It links remote-strike operations to the relevant country and conflict articles instead of adding a separate war for each method. A rigorous dataset would record the operator, weapon type where confirmed, target, civilian-harm assessment and source confidence.
