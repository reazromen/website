# Sudan–South Sudan Border Clashes and Oil-Region Tensions

Sudan and South Sudan share a border shaped by unresolved demarcation, oil infrastructure, migration routes and communities whose lives cross the boundary. After South Sudan became independent in 2011, disputes over territory and oil revenues generated serious tensions, including the 2012 fighting around Heglig. Later episodes must be distinguished from the two countries’ internal wars and from diplomatic disputes that did not involve sustained armed combat.

## The Heglig confrontation

Heglig, an oil-producing area claimed by Sudan and contested by South Sudan, became the focus of direct fighting in 2012. South Sudanese forces occupied the area for a short period, and Sudan responded militarily before South Sudan withdrew. The confrontation raised fears of a return to full-scale war between the neighboring states and disrupted oil production, a major source of revenue for both economies.

The episode occurred soon after independence, when the two governments were still negotiating oil transit fees, citizenship, border demarcation and the status of disputed areas. These disputes were connected to the legacy of Sudan’s north–south civil wars, but the post-independence interstate confrontation had its own parties and timeline.

## Border communities and local armed actors

The wider border region includes Abyei and other contested areas where local communities, security forces and armed groups have been affected by uncertainty over administration and access to land. Not every local clash is an interstate battle. An archive should distinguish clashes between national forces, local communal violence, attacks by non-state armed groups and incidents whose perpetrators remain uncertain.

Oil infrastructure can be a strategic target, but claims about damage, casualties or responsibility require source attribution. The presence of armed forces in a disputed area does not by itself establish who initiated a particular incident or whether it forms part of a broader campaign.

## Regional context

The two states’ internal conflicts affect border security and humanitarian conditions. Sudan’s war since 2023 has added new displacement and instability, while South Sudan continues to face localized armed violence and political fragility. These developments should be cross-linked but not treated as one unified Sudanese war.

## Classification and sources

This entry tracks documented interstate clashes and border incidents where organized armed violence is established. It does not classify all disputes over sovereignty, oil payments or administrative boundaries as armed conflict.
