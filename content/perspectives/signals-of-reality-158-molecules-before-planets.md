Planets feel like the natural home of chemistry.

They provide surfaces.

Atmospheres.

Oceans.

Temperature gradients.

But chemistry starts earlier.

Long before a planet exists, interstellar clouds contain molecules.

Some of them are simple.

Some are surprisingly complex.

A forming planetary system inherits a chemical past.

## Space is sparse, not chemically empty

Interstellar space has extremely low density compared with a laboratory.

Yet enormous volumes and long times create opportunities for reactions.

Dense molecular clouds shield material from some destructive radiation.

Dust grains provide surfaces.

Atoms and molecules collide, stick, react, freeze into ices, and later desorb.

Cold does not mean chemically dead.

It changes which pathways matter.

## Water ice forms before planets

NASA's work on interstellar chemistry describes molecular clouds as reservoirs where water, methanol, ammonia, carbon monoxide, and other species freeze onto dust grains.

These dust grains later participate in star and planet formation.

So a protoplanetary disk does not begin with only atoms.

It inherits molecules and ices from earlier stages.

Some of that material is processed again.

Some may survive.

## Complex organics can arise in clouds

Astronomers have detected many carbon-containing molecules in interstellar environments.

Laboratory experiments and observations show that ultraviolet light, cosmic rays, and grain-surface chemistry can build increasing molecular complexity.

NASA's JWST work has even reported evidence that complex molecules form in dark pre-stellar ices before stars are born.

This does not mean life exists in molecular clouds.

Organic means carbon-containing chemistry, not biological origin.

That distinction is essential.

## Planet formation is chemical inheritance plus reprocessing

As a cloud collapses, conditions change dramatically.

Temperatures rise near the protostar.

Ices evaporate.

New reactions occur.

Disks develop gradients.

Radiation processes material.

Dust grows into larger solids.

Some inherited chemistry survives.

Some is destroyed and rebuilt.

Planetary composition is therefore both ancestral and local.

## A molecule can predate its planet

This is an odd thought.

We often think a planet produces its chemistry.

In reality, some molecular material can be older than the planet that later contains it.

The system boundary changed.

The molecule belonged first to a cloud, then a disk, then perhaps a comet, asteroid, atmosphere, or ocean.

Matter moves across categories we treat as separate objects.

## Prebiotic does not mean pre-life guarantee

Astrochemistry has found molecules relevant to prebiotic chemistry.

That is exciting because it shows some building blocks form without biology.

But the existence of a molecule does not imply a path to life is easy or inevitable.

Life requires organization, sustained networks, replication, evolution, and environmental conditions far beyond simply having organics.

Chemistry expands possibility.

It does not write destiny.

## The Solar System began with memory

When the Sun formed, it inherited a molecular cloud shaped by previous stars and interstellar chemistry.

The disk around it was not chemically blank.

Planet formation rearranged an already historical mixture.

Molecules before planets change the direction of the story.

The planet is not always the beginning of chemistry.

Sometimes the planet is where older chemistry arrives.
