# Drone and Remote-Strike Campaigns

Remote strikes and armed drones have been used across several conflicts, including counterterrorism operations and conventional warfare. The technology changes how forces gather intelligence, select targets and apply force, but the presence of a drone does not itself define a conflict. The same operation may be part of a larger war, a counterterrorism campaign or a single cross-border incident.

## Timeline and actors

A sound record distinguishes armed drones from surveillance systems, identifies the operator where known, dates the strike, documents the target and assesses reported civilian harm. Attribution can be contested, and official claims should be compared with independent investigations. Legal questions include distinction, proportionality, precautions and the sovereignty of the territory where a strike occurs.

## Scope and classification

This is a method-of-warfare index across multiple conflicts, not a standalone war. The archive should link every strike to its primary theatre and avoid combining unrelated operations merely because they use similar technology. Civilian casualty estimates should preserve source, uncertainty and revision history.
