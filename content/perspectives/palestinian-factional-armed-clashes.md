# Palestinian Factional Armed Clashes: Gaza, Political Division, and Civilian Risk

Palestinian political division has at times produced armed clashes between factions and security forces, especially around the struggle for authority between Fatah and Hamas. These episodes are connected to the wider Israeli–Palestinian conflict, but they have distinct parties, triggers and civilian impacts. A careful archive should not describe Palestinian society as a single armed actor or treat political disagreement as proof of participation in violence.

## The 2006–2007 rupture

After Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative election, disputes over security control, government formation and relations with Israel and international actors intensified. Armed confrontations between Hamas and Fatah-linked forces escalated in 2007, culminating in Hamas taking control of Gaza and the Palestinian Authority retaining authority in parts of the West Bank. The fighting caused deaths, injuries and fear among civilians, and left Palestinian governance geographically and institutionally divided.

The episode involved armed factions and security bodies with different chains of command. It should be documented separately from attacks by Palestinian groups against Israeli targets and from Israeli military operations in Gaza. These conflicts interact, but their event attribution is not interchangeable.

## Later episodes and local violence

Gaza has experienced additional internal clashes, including disputes involving armed factions, local families and security authorities. Not every shooting or detention is an armed-conflict event; inclusion requires evidence of organized actors and a defensible threshold. Reports of factional responsibility can be contested, so the archive should identify the source and uncertainty behind each claim.

Political repression, arbitrary detention and attacks on civilians can occur outside the threshold of a sustained armed conflict. Such harms may be important to human-rights documentation even when they belong in a separate category from war. The same principle applies in the West Bank: Fatah, Hamas, other factions, security services and unaffiliated individuals should not be collapsed into one actor.

## Civilian protection and political consequences

Internal fighting places civilians at risk in densely populated areas and weakens public institutions. It can also affect access to healthcare, employment and movement, and discourage political participation. Any account of the conflict should distinguish documented civilian deaths from combatant deaths and should not assign collective responsibility to an entire population.

The political division between Gaza and the West Bank has complicated representation, accountability and prospects for a negotiated settlement. Ending factional armed violence requires accountable security institutions and political processes that allow competition without coercion.

## Scope and sources

This entry covers documented internal Palestinian factional armed clashes, especially the 2006–2007 fighting, while cross-linking to the Gaza wars and the wider Israeli–Palestinian conflict. It is not a claim that all Palestinian political violence forms a separate continuous war.
