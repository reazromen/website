# External Military Support in Yemen: Coalition Intervention and Iranian Links

Yemen’s war has been shaped by external military involvement as well as domestic political fragmentation. A Saudi-led coalition intervened in 2015 in support of Yemen’s internationally recognized government. Iran has been accused by Saudi Arabia, the United States and other governments of providing military assistance to the Houthi movement; the nature and extent of specific transfers and operational links have varied across sources and periods.

## Coalition intervention

The coalition’s campaign included air operations, maritime measures and support to local forces. Member states’ roles were not identical, and the composition and intensity of participation changed over time. Civilian casualties, damage to infrastructure and allegations of violations of international humanitarian law have been documented by UN bodies and independent organizations. Specific incidents should be described with attribution and the evidence available for that event.

## Iranian support and Houthi agency

The Houthi movement has its own political history, leadership and domestic support base. External assistance may have affected its capabilities, but describing the movement only as an Iranian proxy obscures its local origins and decision-making. Evidence about weapons transfers, training or technical support should be distinguished from claims that Iran directed a particular attack. The same standard applies to Saudi, Emirati and other support to Yemeni partners.

## Regional spillover

Yemen’s conflict has intersected with attacks on shipping, cross-border strikes and wider regional confrontation. Those events may require separate incident records, but they should be linked to the primary Yemen conflict rather than assumed to form one continuous regional war.

## Archive treatment

This page is an external-support index. It separates direct military intervention, material support and alleged command relationships. The archive should not count support alone as a distinct conflict and should retain uncertainty where governments’ claims differ.
