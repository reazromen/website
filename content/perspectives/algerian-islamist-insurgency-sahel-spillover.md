# Algeria’s Islamist Insurgency After 2000: From Civil-War Legacy to Sahel Networks

Algeria’s major civil-conflict period began in the 1990s and largely falls outside this archive’s 2000–2026 window. The later period nevertheless included attacks by Islamist armed groups, counterterrorism operations and networks that later operated across the Sahel. The post-2000 record must be dated carefully rather than presented as an unchanged civil war.

## Organizational change

The Armed Islamic Group (GIA) and other formations emerged during Algeria’s 1990s conflict. The Salafist Group for Preaching and Combat (GSPC) later aligned itself with al-Qaeda and adopted the name al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb (AQIM) in 2007. This evolution matters: attacks attributed to an earlier faction should not automatically be assigned to AQIM, and regional affiliates developed their own command and operational patterns.

Algerian security forces pursued armed networks through arrests, raids and military operations. Violence declined in some areas but did not disappear uniformly. Kidnappings and attacks also had cross-border consequences as militant networks moved through the Sahara and connected with conflicts in Mali and neighbouring states.

## Sahel connections

AQIM and splinter organizations became involved in hostage-taking and insurgent campaigns beyond Algeria. Such links are relevant to the wider Sahel conflict, but do not make every Sahel event an Algerian conflict event. The archive should record where an organization operated, which actors fought and what evidence establishes the link.

## Classification and sources

This scope-sensitive entry covers documented post-2000 armed activity linked to Algeria’s Islamist insurgency and transnational evolution, while treating the 1990s war as context. Sources include UN Security Council monitoring reports, International Crisis Group, UCDP and ACLED.
