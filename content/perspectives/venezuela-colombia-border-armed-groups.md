# Venezuela–Colombia Borderland Armed Groups and Cross-Border Violence

The Venezuela–Colombia border is a long, socially connected region where armed organizations, informal economies, migration and state security operations intersect. Colombian armed groups have operated in border departments and, according to multiple reports, some have used Venezuelan territory or developed local presence there. These dynamics do not automatically mean that Venezuela and Colombia are at war with each other.

## Armed organizations and borderland economies

The post-2016 transformation of Colombia’s armed conflict altered the landscape. FARC dissident structures, the National Liberation Army (ELN) and other organizations have competed for influence in areas affected by illicit economies and weak state presence. Their leadership, alliances and territorial control vary by region and time. A single label such as “border armed groups” can conceal different organizations and relationships.

Venezuelan authorities have periodically conducted operations near the frontier, while Colombia has pursued its own security operations. Claims about state collaboration, tolerance or confrontation with particular armed groups can be politically charged and should be tied to named sources. An organization operating across a border is not proof that the two states are direct combatants.

## Civilian impact

Residents can face extortion, recruitment pressure, movement restrictions, displacement and violence. Cross-border displacement may also reflect economic collapse or family ties, so it should not automatically be attributed to a specific armed incident. Event reporting should separate confirmed attacks from allegations and distinguish the organization blamed from the organization independently verified.

## Archive treatment

This page connects Colombia’s actor-specific conflict records to borderland developments in Venezuela. It should not count the border as an independent war unless the evidence supports a distinct campaign. Direct interstate clashes, armed-group operations and criminal activity should be recorded separately, with dates and clear attribution.
