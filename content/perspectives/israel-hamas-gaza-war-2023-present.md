# Israel–Hamas and the War in Gaza: Attacks, Hostilities, and Civilian Catastrophe

The war that began in October 2023 is a major phase of the longer Israeli–Palestinian conflict, but it should be documented as a distinct campaign with its own dates, actors and consequences. On 7 October 2023, Hamas and other Palestinian armed groups attacked communities and military sites in southern Israel, killing civilians and security personnel and taking hostages. Israel launched a large military campaign in Gaza, stating that its aims included dismantling Hamas’s military and governing capacity and recovering hostages.

## Background and escalation

The war emerged from decades of conflict over territory, occupation, security, displacement, blockade, political representation and competing national claims. Hamas has governed Gaza since 2007 and is designated a terrorist organization by the United States, European Union and several other governments. Palestinian civilians are not synonymous with Hamas, just as Israeli civilians are not synonymous with the Israeli government. Those distinctions are essential to accurate reporting and civilian protection.

Israel’s campaign included airstrikes, ground operations, evacuation orders and restrictions affecting movement and access to essential goods. Hamas and other armed groups continued to fight Israeli forces and held hostages; rocket attacks and armed operations formed part of the wider hostilities. The conflict’s phases and the status of any ceasefire or hostage agreement must be dated carefully.

## Civilian harm and humanitarian conditions

Gaza’s civilian population endured mass displacement, deaths and injuries, destruction of housing and public infrastructure, and severe constraints on food, clean water, health care and sanitation. Hospitals and humanitarian operations faced repeated disruption. Israeli hostages and their families also experienced grave harm, uncertainty and trauma. The effects extend beyond immediate casualties to disability, interrupted education, loss of livelihoods, family separation and long-term reconstruction needs.

Reported death tolls and damage assessments differ in methodology, access and verification. The archive should identify the reporting body, date and definition, and distinguish reported figures from independently verified totals. Claims about individual attacks require evidence about the target, circumstances and civilian impact.

## Law, accountability, and political future

International humanitarian law prohibits deliberate attacks on civilians and hostage-taking, and requires all parties to distinguish civilians from combatants and to take feasible precautions. Alleged violations must be investigated with evidence and due process. Legal proceedings and international findings should be described precisely: allegations, provisional measures, arrest warrants and final judgments are not interchangeable.

A lasting resolution would need to address civilian protection, hostages and detainees, governance in Gaza, Palestinian political rights, Israeli security, reconstruction and the broader political future. Military victory alone cannot settle the conflict’s underlying national and territorial questions.
