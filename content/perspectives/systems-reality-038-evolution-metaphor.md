Calling evolution a **continuous production migration** is useful for about five minutes. The comparison can expose structure that ordinary language hides. The problem begins when the comparison stops behaving like a tool and starts behaving like a claim about what the thing literally is.

One useful engineering analogy is a production system that can never be globally shut down for a clean rewrite. Every viable change must pass through a functioning lineage.

## What the metaphor gets right

One useful engineering analogy is a production system that can never be globally shut down for a clean rewrite. Every viable change must pass through a functioning lineage.

This is why engineering metaphors are so attractive. They compress a complicated relationship into something we can manipulate mentally. In the best case, the metaphor produces predictions and sharper questions.

## What it smuggles in

Software migrations are intentional and judged against chosen goals. Evolution has no roadmap, product owner or universal optimization metric; history constrains which changes are reachable.

Engineering words often carry hidden assumptions: a designer, a specification, modular ownership, explicit goals, a stable machine underneath the program. Those assumptions must be tested rather than inherited.

## Return to the physical story

Evolution changes populations across generations through heritable variation, differential reproductive success, drift, migration and other processes. Natural selection has no foresight: variants are filtered in actual environments rather than against a future design document.

Once the mechanism is back in view, the analogy can be kept on a short leash. We can state which relation is genuinely similar and which difference would make the analogy generate a wrong prediction.

## My three tests for a metaphor

1. **What mechanism does this comparison help me see?**
2. **Which engineering assumption does the natural system not share?**
3. **What would I predict incorrectly if I took the comparison literally?**

The third test is the most important. An analogy that cannot fail is decoration, not explanation.

## Beyond the borrowed language

Darwin's descent with modification unified observations across species, geography and fossils; genetics later supplied mechanisms of inheritance that Darwin did not know.

Evolution replaces essence with history: what something is partly reflects the path by which it arrived.

What happens to our idea of good design when there is no designer and no greenfield rewrite?

A good metaphor should eventually make itself less necessary. It gets us close enough to the real system that we can see where the scaffolding ends.
