# Iranian Kurdish Insurgencies and the Iran–PJAK Conflict

Iran’s Kurdish regions have experienced political repression, protest, security operations and episodic armed activity involving Kurdish organizations. One prominent armed actor in the post-2000 period is the Party for a Free Life in Kurdistan (PJAK), which has fought Iranian security forces and has links to the broader Kurdish militant landscape. PJAK is not interchangeable with all Kurdish political organizations, and Kurdish communities in Iran are politically diverse.

## Armed activity and cross-border geography

PJAK emerged in the early 2000s and operated in mountainous areas along the Iran–Iraq border. Iranian authorities have carried out security operations against suspected fighters and bases, while PJAK has claimed or been attributed attacks on Iranian forces. The group’s relationship with the PKK and other Kurdish organizations is often discussed, but organizational connections should not be assumed to mean identical command, objectives or operations.

The border creates practical and political complications. Armed activity may cross or approach the boundary, while Iranian strikes or operations in northern Iraq affect Iraq’s sovereignty and local communities. Those incidents should be recorded with their own dates, locations and target claims rather than treated as evidence of uninterrupted interstate war between Iran and Iraq.

## Political context and civilian rights

Kurdish citizens in Iran have raised concerns over cultural rights, political representation, economic marginalization and security restrictions. Iranian authorities have described armed Kurdish organizations as security threats and have conducted arrests and prosecutions. Political activism, ethnic identity and peaceful protest are not evidence of membership in an armed group. Similarly, attacks by an organization must be attributed to that organization rather than generalized to Kurdish people.

Civilians in border districts can face militarization, displacement, restricted access to land and livelihoods, and risks from cross-border fire. Reliable casualty accounting requires separation of combatants, civilians and people whose status is unknown. Official announcements and claims by armed organizations should be checked against independent reporting where possible.

## How to classify the conflict

The Iran–PJAK conflict is an actor-specific insurgency with periods of differing intensity, not a continuous high-intensity war in every year since 2000. Other Iranian Kurdish organizations and episodes may need separate records if their actors, objectives or timelines differ. The archive should also distinguish this theatre from protests inside Iran and from Iran’s wider confrontation with Israel.

## Scope and sources

This page covers armed confrontation between Iranian state forces and PJAK, plus closely related cross-border incidents where evidence supports the link. It does not treat all Kurdish activism as insurgency or merge every Kurdish organization into one actor.
