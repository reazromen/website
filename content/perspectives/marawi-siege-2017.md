# The Marawi Siege of 2017: Urban Warfare, Displacement, and the Islamic State Connection

The battle for Marawi began in May 2017 after Philippine security forces attempted to capture Isnilon Hapilon, a militant leader associated with the Islamic State. Fighting escalated when militants from several local factions occupied parts of Marawi City in Mindanao. The Philippine government declared martial law across Mindanao and deployed a large military campaign. The battle lasted about five months and ended in October 2017 with the government announcing the deaths of key militant leaders and the city’s liberation.

Marawi should be understood as a discrete urban battle within the wider conflict environment of Mindanao, not as a synonym for the Moro autonomy struggle. The militants included members of the Maute group and other pro-Islamic State networks. Their project differed from the political programme pursued by the principal Moro peace-process actors.

## Urban combat and the city’s destruction

Fighting in densely populated neighbourhoods created acute civilian risks. Militants used buildings and urban terrain for defence, while government forces used ground operations and airstrikes. Residents fled, many carrying few possessions. Large areas were severely damaged, and unexploded ordnance and damaged infrastructure complicated later returns.

Urban warfare creates problems for both combat operations and post-conflict recovery. A building may be used by fighters while nearby homes contain civilians; the legal and humanitarian implications depend on the circumstances and precautions taken. Broad descriptions such as “liberated” or “destroyed” cannot replace a record of civilian deaths, damage, missing people and unexploded hazards.

## Displacement and the aftermath

The siege displaced a large share of Marawi’s population and affected surrounding communities. Return was not immediate for everyone. Reconstruction, property claims, compensation, livelihood recovery and the removal of explosive remnants took time. Displaced households may have been counted at different points by different agencies, so figures need a date and should distinguish people from families and temporary evacuation from prolonged displacement.

The effects also extended beyond the city. Security operations, checkpoints and the spread of fears about Islamic State-linked recruitment influenced wider Mindanao. The battle became a reference point for counterterrorism policy and regional security cooperation, while raising questions about the treatment of Muslim communities and the long-term consequences of emergency powers.

## Armed actors and attribution

The Islamic State connection was important, but it should be described precisely. Local militants pledged allegiance or aligned themselves with the group; this did not mean that every local armed actor in Mindanao was under one command. Likewise, a shared religious identity does not establish membership in an extremist organization.

The Philippine state has a duty to protect civilians and investigate credible allegations of abuse. Militants also remain responsible for their conduct, including hostage-taking, attacks on civilians and other violations. These responsibilities do not cancel one another. A balanced archive should distinguish documented incidents from claims made during active combat, when independent access is limited.

## Classification in the conflict archive

Marawi is best represented as a discrete siege and urban battle, connected to the wider Philippine campaign against Islamic State-linked groups and to the broader Mindanao conflict. It should not be counted as an additional full war if the underlying battle events are already included in another conflict record. The archive should track battle deaths, civilian deaths, displacement, destruction and post-conflict recovery separately.

UCDP’s annual datasets provide a framework for organized conflict and violence against civilians, but a city siege also needs detailed historical and humanitarian sources. The archive’s 2026 status concerns current event coverage and does not alter the historical dates of the 2017 battle.
