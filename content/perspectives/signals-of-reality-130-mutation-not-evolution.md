A DNA sequence changes.

That is mutation.

A population changes genetically across generations.

That is evolution.

The two are deeply connected.

They are not synonyms.

Mutation supplies new variants.

Evolution describes what happens to heritable variation through population processes over time.

## Mutation begins the possibility

A point mutation changes one nucleotide.

An insertion adds sequence.

A deletion removes it.

Duplications, inversions, transpositions, and other genomic changes create larger variation.

These events can occur in germline cells and become heritable.

Most mutations do not arrive with an evolutionary destiny.

They are raw variation.

What happens next depends on context.

## Selection acts on consequences

If a heritable variant affects reproductive success, natural selection can change its frequency.

Beneficial in one environment.

Harmful in another.

Neutral under some conditions.

The mutation itself is a molecular event.

Selection is a population process.

A mutation can occur once and disappear.

Another can spread.

Another can drift for generations without strong selection.

Evolution concerns these frequency changes and lineage histories.

## Drift needs no advantage

Finite populations sample descendants imperfectly.

By chance, some variants become more common and others disappear.

This is genetic drift.

It can be especially strong in small populations.

Drift is important because not every evolutionary change is adaptive.

A variant can spread without being better.

A useful variant can disappear before selection has much chance to act.

Population history matters.

## Migration changes populations too

When individuals move and reproduce in another population, they move alleles.

Gene flow can introduce variation without a new mutation occurring locally.

Two populations can become more similar because of migration.

Or diverge when gene flow is limited.

Evolution is therefore broader than mutation plus selection.

Population structure is part of the process.

## Recombination rearranges existing variation

Sexual reproduction creates new combinations of alleles.

No new nucleotide mutation is required for a new genotype to appear.

Recombination can bring beneficial variants together or break combinations apart.

Again, evolution has multiple engines.

Mutation is essential as the ultimate source of new sequence variation, but much evolutionary dynamics concerns reshuffling and changing frequencies of existing variants.

## Somatic mutation is another category

Your body accumulates mutations in somatic cells.

Those mutations can contribute to cancer or cellular mosaicism.

They are biological change.

They usually do not enter population evolution through reproduction.

This is another reason the words should remain distinct.

Not every mutation is evolutionary in the population-genetic sense.

## Evolution can occur with no new mutation during the interval

Suppose a population already contains several alleles.

The environment changes.

One existing allele becomes advantageous.

Its frequency rises over generations.

Evolution occurred even if no relevant new mutation appeared during that period.

Standing genetic variation can power rapid adaptation.

## Clear vocabulary prevents bad arguments

People sometimes say:

evolution is random because mutation is random.

That confuses one process with the entire system.

Mutation with respect to future adaptive need is not directed in the simple Lamarckian sense.

Selection is non-random with respect to differential reproductive consequences.

Drift adds stochasticity.

Migration and recombination add more structure.

Mutation is not the same as evolution.

It creates possibilities.

Evolution is what populations do with inherited possibilities across time.
