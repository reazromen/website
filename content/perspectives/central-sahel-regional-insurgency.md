# The Central Sahel Insurgency: A Regional Conflict System, Not One War

The Central Sahel is a connected security region in which armed groups, state forces, local self-defence formations and civilians move across the borders of Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger. The term “Central Sahel insurgency” helps explain regional networks and policy. It is not a substitute for documenting distinct national conflicts, actors and events.

## Regional development

Mali’s 2012 crisis and subsequent intervention reshaped armed politics across the region. Jihadist organizations expanded into central Mali and neighbouring countries, using mobility, coercion and cross-border routes. Jama’at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin and Islamic State Sahel Province have competed and operated in overlapping areas; their local affiliations and command relationships change over time.

State responses also cross borders. Joint-force initiatives, foreign military operations and later changes in security partnerships affected all three countries, but their mandates and participants differed. A regional framework cannot prove that all violence was centrally coordinated.

## Civilian harm and local conflict

Communities face raids, attacks on transport and security posts, forced recruitment, extortion, displacement and restrictions on markets or grazing. Local disputes over land, water and authority can intersect with insurgency. Treating every communal clash as jihadist violence erases local causes. Allegations of abuses by armed groups and state-aligned forces require independent investigation.
