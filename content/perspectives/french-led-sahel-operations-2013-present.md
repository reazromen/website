# French-Led Sahel Operations: Serval, Barkhane and the Regional Insurgencies

French military operations in the Sahel were closely tied to armed conflict in Mali and the expansion of jihadist insurgencies across neighbouring countries. Operation Serval began in January 2013 in response to advances by armed groups in northern Mali. It was followed by Operation Barkhane in 2014, which adopted a wider regional counterterrorism mission involving several Sahel states.

## From Mali to a regional campaign

Serval helped halt the advance of armed groups toward southern Mali and supported the recapture of major northern towns. Barkhane sought to pursue jihadist networks across Mali, Niger, Chad and Burkina Faso, working with national forces and regional partners. The campaign operated in a landscape where armed groups, local grievances, communal defence formations and state security institutions interacted in complex ways.

Military operations did not resolve the political and governance problems that contributed to insecurity. Violence expanded in parts of the central Sahel, while relations between France and several host governments deteriorated after coups and political realignments. France ended its military presence in Mali and later withdrew from other countries in the region. These changes should be dated by country and mission rather than summarized as one simultaneous regional event.

## Why the intervention is not a separate war

French operations were an external military layer in the conflicts in Mali and neighbouring states. They affected the capabilities of armed groups and the tactics of national forces, but the underlying conflicts had local parties and histories. Counting Barkhane as a separate war on top of each national conflict risks duplicating the same events.
