# Red Sea Attacks and Houthi Regional Operations Since 2023

Since late 2023, the Houthi movement in Yemen has carried out attacks involving commercial shipping and military targets in and around the Red Sea, Gulf of Aden and adjacent waters. The movement has publicly linked many attacks to the war in Gaza and to pressure on Israel and its partners. The attacks have connected Yemen’s conflict to international maritime security, but they should not be treated as a separate war without defining the parties and threshold used.

## A maritime theatre linked to Yemen

The Houthis emerged from Yemen’s domestic political and armed conflict. Their ability to threaten shipping reflects both their military capabilities and the strategic geography of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and Red Sea routes. States have responded through naval deployments, interception operations and strikes on Houthi-controlled targets. The participants and legal explanations vary by operation, so each event should be dated and attributed.

Not every reported incident has the same level of certainty. A claim of responsibility, a government announcement and an independently confirmed impact are different types of evidence. The archive should distinguish attacks attempted from attacks that caused damage or casualties, and should state when responsibility is disputed.

## Regional escalation and classification

These events overlap with Yemen’s civil war and the wider Israel–Iran confrontation. They also involve international shipping and naval forces whose participation does not necessarily mean that every country is a party to one continuous armed conflict. The term “proxy war” alone cannot establish command responsibility or explain each attack.

## Archive treatment

This entry is a linked maritime campaign index. It connects the Yemen civil-war and external-support essays with dated Red Sea incidents and responses. It should not double-count the same strike or interception under multiple theatres. The scope remains sensitive to new evidence and the evolving security situation.
