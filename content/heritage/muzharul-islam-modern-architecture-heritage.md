Heritage is often associated with distant centuries: forts, temples, mosques and archaeological ruins. But the built environment of the twentieth century also records how a society imagined education, public life, climate, materials and modernity. Modern architecture can carry historical value even when its buildings are comparatively recent.

Bangladesh submitted **The Architectural Works of Muzharul Islam: an Outstanding Contribution to the Modern Movement in South Asia** to UNESCO's Tentative List in 2023. The proposal identifies a group of works, including buildings at the University of Dhaka, as part of his architectural legacy.

## Architecture as a public idea

Muzharul Islam's work is important to discussions of modern architecture in Bangladesh because it invites attention to more than façades. A building organises movement, light, shade, gathering and the relationship between people and their surroundings. Plans, material choices and the treatment of the site can express a view of how public institutions should function.

To study modern architectural heritage, researchers need drawings, construction records, photographs, oral histories and evidence of later alterations. Those records help distinguish the architect's original intentions from the way a building has been modified or used over time.

## Conservation without turning buildings into replicas

A modern building may depend on materials and systems that age differently from older brick structures. Conservation choices therefore need to be based on the actual building: its fabric, structure, use, repairs and technical requirements. Replacing every worn surface may make a building look fresh while removing evidence of its history; leaving a building without maintenance can also put its fabric at risk.

The goal is not to freeze architecture at one date. It is to understand which features carry significance and how a building can remain safe, usable and legible without losing the qualities that make it worth keeping.

## Status and scope

The Muzharul Islam proposal is on Bangladesh's UNESCO Tentative List. It is not a final World Heritage inscription. The listing offers a framework for understanding the proposed group of works, but it does not replace detailed building records or the views of people who use and care for each site.

This article is a source-led introduction, not an architectural survey or an assessment of the present condition of any listed building.
