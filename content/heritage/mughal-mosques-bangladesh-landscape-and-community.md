Historic mosques are often discussed through their domes, arches and ornament. In Bangladesh, the setting is equally important. Rivers and changing channels shaped settlement, travel, trade and the locations where religious institutions developed. Architecture grew within that geography rather than apart from it.

Bangladesh submitted **Mughal Mosques in Bangladesh** to UNESCO's Tentative List in 2023. The submission describes mosque-building as connected to the formation and expansion of settlements, including places that were forested or shaped by active river systems. It also discusses the relationship between mosques, shrines, local communities and patterns of religious life.

## Architecture and the delta

The submission points to variations in mosque form across the country, including single-domed and three-domed buildings. It relates some forms to local materials and the demands of coastal and riverine environments. Such patterns are useful starting points for comparison, but they do not mean every building from the period can be explained by geography alone.

Construction history, patronage, repairs, inscriptions and local practice must be examined at each site. Architectural similarity may suggest connections, but it cannot by itself prove who built a mosque, when a change occurred or how a community understood the building.

## Buildings are not frozen in time

Mosques may remain places of worship while also being recognised as historic structures. That continued use is part of the heritage context. It can influence access, maintenance and how conservation decisions are discussed with the people who use the site.

A careful account avoids presenting a religious building only as a picturesque object. It recognises the faith and community practices associated with it, while using documented evidence for claims about dates, design and historical events.

## A proposal, not a final designation

The UNESCO entry is a Tentative List submission, dated 17 May 2023. It is not an inscription on the World Heritage List. That distinction is essential when describing the international status of the buildings.

This article summarises the published proposal and adds editorial interpretation; it is not a site survey or a statement on behalf of local worshippers.
