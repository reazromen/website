Historic architecture is often explained through style: arches, walls, gateways and ornament. But the Mughal forts associated with Dhaka also tell a story about geography. The Bengal delta was shaped by rivers, canals, seasonal flooding and shifting channels. Fortification here had to respond to water as well as land.

UNESCO's Tentative List entry for **Mughal Forts on Fluvial Terrains in Dhaka** groups four sites: Hajiganj Fort, Sonakanda Fort and Idrakpur Fort, alongside Lalbagh Fort. The entry describes the first three as naval forts and Lalbagh as a palace-fort complex. It presents them as a connected system associated with the Mughal administrative and military presence in Bengal.

## Architecture adapted to local conditions

The Tentative List account describes the use of brick, lime mortar and plaster in a region where stone was less readily available. It also explains how the forts' placement related to river routes and the strategic importance of Dhaka. These features are not merely a regional variation on a familiar Mughal style; they show buildings responding to local materials, waterways and military needs.

The river landscape has changed since the forts were built. UNESCO's submission notes that the river channels have shifted and that dense urban development has altered the wider setting. This matters because a fort is more than its surviving walls. Its relationship to water, movement and surrounding settlement is part of how its original purpose can be understood.

## Read the status accurately

The site was submitted to UNESCO's Tentative List on 17 May 2023. A Tentative List is not the same as inscription on the World Heritage List. The distinction matters: it identifies a proposal a country may consider for future nomination, not a final international designation.

The value of this group is in reading the forts together and in their landscape. Isolated photographs can show a gateway or bastion; the historical record helps explain why the sites were positioned where they were and how they related to the riverine delta.

This article summarises the UNESCO Tentative List submission and adds editorial interpretation. It is not a site survey or a substitute for conservation reports and local research.
