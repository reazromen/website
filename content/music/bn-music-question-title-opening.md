When I think about System of a Down's *Question!*, the openness of the title is a useful place to begin. A question does not always exist to produce an immediate answer. Sometimes its job is to make a familiar thing visible again. A reading of a song can remain open in the same way.

If I reduce a song to a statement, I shrink the work done by melody, rhythm, arrangement, and time. While listening, I can follow where a sound appears, what kind of waiting comes before it, and how its meaning changes when it returns. Claiming that one interpretation is the artist's final intention would be a much larger claim.

The question for this note is how a song can preserve uncertainty. Refusing a final answer can be vagueness, or it can create deliberate open space. To distinguish the two, I have to return to the recording and look for evidence in the sound itself.

That is part of the value of revisiting a song. If the first interpretation is not treated as the last word, another reading remains possible. The question then becomes not only a title, but a method of listening.
